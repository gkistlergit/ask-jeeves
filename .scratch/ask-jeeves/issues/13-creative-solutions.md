# Creative solutions: how they're presented
Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 12

## Question

How does the memo present at least one creative, non-obvious solution? Settle the label, the risk and enforceability rating, how many to give, and the guardrails so it is never presented as a sure thing.

## Answer

Resolved 2026-10-20. Grant accepted the recommendations ("go for recs on tickets 09, 11 and 13"). The baseline is the prototype memo's Creative option.

- **How many:** at least 1, at most 2 per memo, at every depth.
- **Label and shape:**
  ```
  ## Creative option
  **Creative, not standard practice.** <the idea in 2–3 sentences>
  Why it might work: <one line> [n]
  **Risk:** Low | Medium | High: <what could backfire>
  **Enforceability:** Likely | Uncertain | Unlikely: <why> [n]
  Run it past a licensed attorney before using it.
  ```
- **Non-obvious test:** it can't just restate a Recommendation such as "negotiate the clause shorter." It should use a lever most people miss. For example: trade a non-compete for paid garden leave, attach a schedule of prior inventions, use a missing DTSA notice as negotiating leverage, or ask for the non-compete to be paid only if enforced.
- **Guardrails:**
  - It must be lawful and fit the safeguards: no deception, nothing that involves data or evidence, and no evading a valid covenant (ticket 15).
  - Every legal premise carries a citation. A purely practical idea is tagged *(practical)*.
  - It fits the mode: employer-side or employee-side.
  - It's never called a sure thing, and "only a court decides" goes in Enforceability where that's true.
