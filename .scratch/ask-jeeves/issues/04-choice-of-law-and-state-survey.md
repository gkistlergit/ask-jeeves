## Question

How do courts decide which state's law governs an employment covenant or trade-secret dispute, current as of October 2026? Cover Restatement (Second) Conflict of Laws section 187 and section 196, how Indiana and South Carolina courts apply them, forum-selection clauses, and the states with statutes that void out-of-state choice of law or forum for employees (CA, MA, CO, WA, MN, others). Cover remote workers, where the employee lives versus where they work, and where events happened. Then give a 50-state plus DC table of non-compete status (banned, banned below a wage threshold, notice rules, limited), each row sourced and dated. What signals make the governing state uncertain?

## Answer

See `research/04-choice-of-law.md`. It went through research, an independent verification, and a follow-up check of 13 rows that hadn't been verified (2026-10-20). No fabricated authority was found.

- **Part A (choice of law):** 46 claims confirmed, 17 corrected, 5 UNVERIFIED. It covers Restatement (Second) §§ 187, 188 and 196 as Indiana and South Carolina courts apply them, plus forum clauses and the anti-waiver statutes (including CA § 925, FL CHOICE Act, MA, CO, WA, MN). *Aprio v. Alexeev* (Del. Ch., 2026-10-16) is confirmed.
- **10-step primary/backup procedure:** it stands, with three edits. Step 3 is the Florida wage test, using the employer's county. Step 7 says SC courts may apply the law of the place where the contract was signed. Step 9 says WA, OR, LA and TN reach existing agreements or pending suits.
- **Part B (51-row survey):** 25 jurisdictions changed in 2025–26 (WA near-total ban effective 2027-06-30, WY general ban, FL CHOICE Act, TN $70,000 floor from 2026-07-01, VA, IL, and 16 healthcare bans including IN). 15 of the 25 change rows needed corrections. In the follow-up, 5 rows were confirmed (including SC), 4 corrected (NE, NM, NY, RI) and 4 left UNVERIFIED (AL, AK, GA, MS) because the official sites couldn't be reached.
- **Caution for the build:** treat Part B as a flagging aid, not authority. For any state other than IN or SC, research its current law at the chosen depth (ticket 10).
