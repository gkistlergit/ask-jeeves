# Insider theft of IT assets, data and trade secrets: current law
Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by:
Findings: research/01-insider-theft.md

## Question

What is the law, current as of October 2026, on employees or insiders taking trade secrets, data or IT assets, under federal law, Indiana and South Carolina? Cover civil and criminal claims, each element and remedy, what the employer must do to protect a trade secret, the DTSA whistleblower-immunity notice, computer-access statutes after Van Buren, conversion and treble-damage statutes, and preemption or displacement of other claims. List the facts that most often decide outcomes, for both the employer and the employee side. Flag every 2025–2026 change.

## Answer

See `research/01-insider-theft.md`. It went through research plus an independent verification pass on 2026-10-20: all 61 authorities exist, 12 claims were corrected, 7 were upgraded to verified, and 14 remain UNVERIFIED. No fabricated authority was found.

- **CHANGED 2025–26:** in *NRA Group v. Durenleau* (3d Cir., amended opinion 154 F.4th 153; cert. denied 2026-03-30, No. 25-1033), breaking a computer-use policy without code-based hacking isn't a CFAA violation, and passwords alone aren't trade secrets. This extends *Van Buren*.
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** Indiana P.L.111-2026 (SEA 119, effective 2026-07-01) adds cell phones to the computer-network definition in computer trespass (IC 35-43-2-3).
- No 2025–26 change was found to the DTSA, the EEA, the Indiana UTSA or the SC Trade Secrets Act. Not checked: federal amendments at OLRC (site down) and the SC 2026 session.
