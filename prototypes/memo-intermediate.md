# Jeeves memo: Brightline Analytics offer letter
> Not legal advice. Jeeves spots issues to raise with a licensed attorney; it is not a lawyer.
Mode: Employee · Applied state: Indiana (why: Indiana employer and office, and the letter picks Indiana law) · Depth: Intermediate, quick-verified · Reference files current as of 2026-10-20 · Prepared 2026-10-20

## Bottom line
- The non-compete and non-solicit are far broader than Indiana courts usually enforce, and those courts strike words rather than rewrite them [1][3][4]. That helps you in court, but it won't stop a demand letter.
- The invention clause and the laptop deduction are likeliest to cost you something; negotiate both before signing.
- The October 9, 2026 sign-by date has passed; confirm the offer still stands before countering.

## State applied and why
Indiana. Brightline is in Indianapolis, you'd work in its office there three days a week, and §8 picks Indiana law and Marion County courts. Courts don't always honor a choice-of-law clause, but every known contact points to Indiana. If you live or work remotely in another state, its law could affect the non-compete and pay rules.
Confirm the applied state with a licensed attorney before relying on this.

## Risks
| # | Issue (clause) | Likelihood it matters | What could go wrong later |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Non-compete (§3) | High: 18 months, nationwide, your whole field | A future employer backs out after a demand letter. Brightline must prove the time, activity and geography are reasonable [1][2]; nationwide and "any capacity" bans have failed [3][5]. The FTC rule is not in effect [6]. |
| 2 | Non-solicit (§4) | Medium: 24 months, every customer and colleague | A former client calls and you must say no. A ban covering "any individual employed" failed in *Heraeus* [1], as did an all-customers clause in *Clark's Sales* [4]. |
| 3 | Confidentiality (§5) | Low: ordinary, but no end date and no whistleblower notice | Every non-public fact stays secret forever. You can still report suspected violations confidentially to officials or a lawyer, and without the notice Brightline loses DTSA exemplary damages and fees [8]. |
| 4 | Inventions (§6) | High: covers off-hours and unrelated work, and Exhibit A is blank | Brightline claims a side project or code you wrote before joining. A signed writing is enough to transfer copyrights and patents [9][10], and Indiana has no statute protecting personal inventions [14, UNVERIFIED]. |
| 5 | Laptop deduction (§7a) | Medium: only if the laptop is late | Brightline docks your final check. Indiana deductions need a signed, revocable wage assignment for a listed purpose, and unreturned property isn't one [12, UNVERIFIED]. Exempt-salary rules list no equipment deduction [11]. Keeping the laptop risks up to three times damages [13, UNVERIFIED]. |
| 6 | Phone wipe (§7b) | Medium: you consent in advance to a full wipe | A wipe at exit takes your photos and two-factor apps. |
| 7 | Signing bonus (§1) | Medium: full repayment even after a layoff | You're laid off in month 11 and owe $10,000. Taking it from final pay hits the same deduction limits [11][12, UNVERIFIED]. |

> "whether or not related to the Company's business"

*In plain terms:* anything you create while employed is Brightline's, even an unrelated weekend project.

> "solicit or accept business from any Company customer"

*In plain terms:* for two years you can't take work from any Brightline customer, even one who calls you first.

## Recommendations
1. Before signing: ask for 6 to 12 months, limited to the region, customers and roles you actually cover, or trade it away (see Creative option).
2. Before signing: cover only customers you personally served in your last 12 months (the FTC's Gateway limit [7]), drop "accept," and limit no-hire to people you managed.
3. On the job: keep company data off personal accounts, as §7(c) requires. Before signing, ask for the §1833(b) notice [8].
4. Before signing: limit it to work related to Brightline's business or made on its time or equipment, and list your prior projects on Exhibit A.
5. Before signing: replace the deduction with return-or-invoice. When leaving: return the laptop in person and get a receipt.
6. Before signing: limit it to a work profile and a company-data-only wipe, or ask for a company phone. On the job: keep backups.
7. Before signing: pro-rate the repayment and exclude a layoff or firing without cause.

## Creative option
**Creative, not standard practice.** Offer to trade the non-compete for a narrow customer non-solicit. If Brightline refuses, don't help it split §3 into divisible pieces: Indiana courts only strike words, so an overbroad clause written as one block tends to fall entirely [1][4]. **Risk:** a void clause can still scare off employers and cost you legal fees, and you will have signed it. **Enforceability:** Indiana enforces covenants tied to where and with whom you worked [2]; the nationwide clause is likely void, but only a court decides that [3][5].

## Questions for a licensed attorney
1. Could another state's law govern §3 or §7 if I work remotely from there?
2. Could a court strike parts of §3 and enforce the rest?
3. Does §6 reach my existing side projects if Exhibit A stays blank?
4. Can Brightline lawfully take the laptop cost or the signing bonus from my final paycheck?
5. What wording would limit a phone wipe to company data?

## Adjacent issues flagged
- Wage-hour: the annual bonus is at Brightline's "sole discretion."
- Wage-hour: no reimbursement for business use of your phone.
- Data privacy: phone management software may see personal data.
- Venue: no arbitration; disputes go to Marion County courts.
