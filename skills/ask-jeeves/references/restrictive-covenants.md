# Restrictive covenants: reference

> For issue-spotting only. Not legal advice. Every claim cites its source; [UNVERIFIED] marks claims that could not be confirmed.

Covers non-competes, customer and employee non-solicits, no-hire clauses, confidentiality agreements (NDAs) and garden leave. Indiana Code cites were checked against the official 2026 IGA download on 2026-10-20.

## Federal

### FTC non-compete rule: gone
- The FTC rule page says the rule is not in effect and not enforceable. [cite: FTC Noncompete Rule page, https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/noncompete-rule]
- Courts ruled against it in 2024: N.D. Tex. set it aside nationwide on 2024-08-20 (*Ryan, LLC v. FTC*); M.D. Fla. ruled against it on 2024-08-15 (*Properties of the Villages v. FTC*). [cite: FTC Noncompete Rule page, https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/noncompete-rule; FTC release 2025-09-05, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** on 2025-09-05 the Commission voted 3-1 to dismiss its appeals (5th Cir. No. 24-10951; 11th Cir. No. 24-13102) and accede to vacatur. [cite: FTC release 2025-09-05, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule]
- The Fifth Circuit clerk dismissed No. 24-10951 under FRAP 42(b) on 2025-09-08. [cite: 5th Cir. clerk's order (certified copy, U.S. Chamber), https://www.uschamber.com/assets/documents/Order-Dismissing-Appeal-Ryan-LLC-v.-FTC-Fifth-Circuit.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** a final rule removed and reserved 16 CFR Part 910, effective **2026-02-12** (FR Doc. 2026-02866, 91 FR 6507). The FTC skipped notice and comment as a ministerial conforming step (5 U.S.C. 553(b)(B), 553(d)(3)). [cite: 91 FR 6507, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/xml/2026/02/12/2026-02866.xml; eCFR version history, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/versions/title-16.json?part=910]
- No enacted federal statute bans non-competes (negative finding). [cite: research/02-restrictive-covenants.md, F22]

### FTC case-by-case enforcement under Section 5 (CHANGED 2025–26)
- After the rule, the FTC acts case by case under Section 5 of the FTC Act: consent orders, warning letters, an RFI and a workshop. [cite: FTC Noncompete tag listing, https://www.ftc.gov/nonmerger/noncompete]
- Joint Labor Task Force launched 2025-02-26. [cite: FTC Noncompete tag listing, https://www.ftc.gov/nonmerger/noncompete]
- **Gateway Services (pet cremation):** proposed 2025-09-04; final Decision and Order (Docket C-4825) issued 2025-11-25.
  - Targeted near-universal one-year nationwide non-competes; Gateway must stop enforcing them, notify employees, and not enter new ones except in limited cases. [cite: FTC Gateway release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-takes-action-protect-workers-noncompete-agreements]
  - Customer non-solicits survive only for customers the employee dealt with or served in the last 12 months of employment. The 12 months is a look-back window, not the non-solicit's length. [cite: FTC Gateway release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-takes-action-protect-workers-noncompete-agreements]
  - Order runs 10 years (§ IX). Exclusions: equity-linked directors, officers and senior employees; sale-of-business covenants where the seller had pre-existing equity; a nonpublic list of employees. [cite: Gateway final D&O, https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/gateway_decision_order.pdf]
- **RFI on employee non-competes:** 2025-09-04, docket FTC-2025-0463-0001, comments closed 2025-11-03; aimed partly at future enforcement. [cite: FTC RFI release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-issues-request-information-employee-noncompete-agreements]
- **Healthcare warning letters, 2025-09-10:** sent to healthcare employers and staffing firms; they target non-competes that are "unjustified, overbroad, or otherwise unfair or anticompetitive," with focus on nurses and physicians. [cite: FTC release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-chairman-ferguson-issues-noncompete-warning-letters-healthcare-employers-staffing-companies]
- **Adamas (building services) B2B no-hire:** proposed 2025-12-19, final 2026-02-12. It voids no-hire clauses binding building owners (NJ/NYC) and requires notice to customers and employees. B2B no-hire clauses are now an FTC target. [cite: FTC Adamas release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-continues-enforcement-action-streak-against-anticompetitive-no-hire-agreements]
- Workshop on anticompetitive non-competes held 2026-01-27. [cite: FTC Noncompete tag listing, https://www.ftc.gov/nonmerger/noncompete]
- **Rollins (Orkin and others):** proposed 2026-04-15; final D&O (Docket C-4835) issued 2026-06-22.
  - Bars entering, keeping or enforcing non-competes for 18,000+ employees, with notice; the agreements typically ran 2 years within about 75 miles of 700+ locations. Same-day warning letters went to 13 other pest-control firms. [cite: FTC Rollins release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/ftc-takes-action-against-noncompete-agreements-securing-protections-workers]
  - Runs 10 years (§ IX). "Covered Employee" excludes equity-eligible directors, officers and senior leaders (branch staff are not senior leaders). Sale-of-business non-competes allowed where the seller had pre-existing equity. [cite: Rollins final D&O, https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/251_0011_rollins_do_public.pdf]
  - Non-solicits survive but may not bar general advertising or answering customer-initiated inquiries (§ II.D). Rollins must stop collecting non-compete fees and penalties. [cite: Rollins final D&O, https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/251_0011_rollins_do_public.pdf]
- Mortgage Connect warning letter, 2026-05-08. No later items appear on the FTC Noncompete tag after 2026-06-22 (not proof of no action). [cite: FTC Noncompete tag listing, https://www.ftc.gov/nonmerger/noncompete?page=1]

### Federal statutes that shape NDAs and covenants
- **DTSA notice, 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)(3):** any employee contract on trade secrets or confidential information made or updated after 2016-05-11 must give notice of whistleblower immunity (a policy cross-reference works). Without it, no exemplary damages or fees against that employee. [cite: 18 U.S.C. § 1833, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- **DTSA injunctions, 18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)(3)(A):** no injunction may "prevent a person from entering into an employment relationship." Conditions need evidence of threatened misappropriation and may not conflict with state restraint-of-trade law. [cite: 18 U.S.C. § 1836, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1836]
- **DTSA remedies:** exemplary damages up to 2x for willful and malicious misappropriation, fees available; 3-year limitations from discovery (§ 1836(d)). [cite: 18 U.S.C. § 1836, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1836]
- **Speak Out Act, 42 U.S.C. § 19403:** pre-dispute NDAs and non-disparagement clauses are not judicially enforceable in sexual assault or harassment disputes; trade-secret protection is preserved (§ 19403(d)). [cite: 42 U.S.C. § 19403, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/19403]
- **SEC Rule 21F-17(a):** no one may impede direct communication with SEC staff about possible securities violations, including by enforcing a confidentiality agreement. [cite: 17 CFR 240.21F-17, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.21F-17]

## Indiana

### Physician non-compete statute, IC 25-22.5-5.5
- The chapter applies to physician non-competes originally entered into on or after 2020-07-01 (§ 1). [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Five required terms (§ 2(a)):** to be enforceable, the agreement must include all five:
  - copies of departure notices sent to patients seen in the prior 2 years (patient names redacted);
  - giving the physician's contact and location information to those patients on request;
  - access to or copies of those patients' records with patient consent;
  - an option to buy a complete release at a reasonable price (declining it does not limit the employer's equitable remedies);
  - records in a format not materially different from how they are kept (paper or PDF suffices).
  - [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Unenforceable after certain exits (§ 2(b), from 2023-07-01):** employer fires without cause; physician quits for cause; or the contract expired with both sides' obligations met. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2(b) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Primary care ban (§ 2.5):** a primary care physician and an employer may not enter a non-compete; does not apply to agreements originally entered into before 2023-07-01. Primary care = family medicine, general pediatric medicine or internal medicine (§ 1.5). [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2.5 and -1.5 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Buyout negotiation and mediation (§ 2.6, agreements from 2023-07-01):** employer must negotiate the buyout price in good faith. If no agreement, either side may serve notice of mediation within 35 days of the physician's buyout election; mediation must end within 45 days of that notice. Mediator costs split equally unless agreed otherwise. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2.6 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- The chapter does not limit other negotiated terms or other legal or equitable remedies (§ 4). [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-4 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]

### Hospital physician ban: SEA 475, P.L. 207-2025 (CHANGED 2025–26)
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** SEA 475 was signed 2025-05-06, effective 2025-07-01, and codified as new IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.1 to -1.4, -1.6, -1.7 and -2.3. [cite: SEA 475 bill page, https://iga.in.gov/legislative/2025/bills/senate/475/details; IC 25-22.5-5.5 history lines (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Ban (§ 2.3):** a physician and a hospital, a hospital's parent company, an affiliated manager of a hospital or a hospital system may not enter a non-compete on or after 2025-07-01; a violating agreement is "void and unenforceable." Pre-2025-07-01 agreements are excluded. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2.3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Who counts:** "hospital" uses IC 16-18-2-179(b), which excludes freestanding health facilities and physician offices that don't regularly keep bed patients. "Hospital system" = a parent of at least one hospital, or a hospital, plus entities affiliated through ownership, governance or membership. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.2, -1.3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25; IC 16-18-2-179(b) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/16]
- **Broad definition (§ 1.4(a), used only for § 2.3):** any contract term that restricts or penalizes post-employment practice, including:
  - a bar on practicing with a new employer;
  - requiring employer consent, or submission to equitable relief, to practice with a new employer;
  - indirect restrictions that limit or deter practice with a new employer;
  - stay-or-pay: penalties or repayment of bonuses, training costs or similar payments for a physician employed at least 3 years, based solely or primarily on leaving to practice elsewhere.
  - [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.4(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Exclusions (§ 1.4(b)):** NDAs protecting confidential business information or trade secrets; non-solicits of current employees lasting no more than 1 year that don't restrict patient interactions, referrals, clinical collaboration or professional relationships; and bona fide sale-of-business covenants where the physician owns more than 50%. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.4(b) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Grandfathering (§ 1.6):** "originally entered into" means first signing; amendments and renewals don't reset the date. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.6 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Executive carve-out (§ 1.7(b)):** solely executive or managerial work with no direct patient care is not "practice of medicine." [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.7 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Not covered:** non-hospital employers of specialists (still subject to § 2), and PAs, APRNs and nurses (the chapter addresses physicians only). [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5 scope (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- No 2026 change: every section's history shows only P.L. 93-2020, P.L. 165-2023 and P.L. 207-2025. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5 history lines (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]

### Reasonableness test (common law)
- Covenants are disfavored restraints of trade, strictly construed against the employer, who must first show a legitimate protectable interest. [cite: *Heraeus Medical, LLC v. Zimmer, Inc.* (Ind. 2019), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html]
- The employer then must show reasonableness in time, activity and geography; it is a question of law and the employer's burden. Customer goodwill is protectable. Physician covenants get particularly careful scrutiny but are not per se void. [cite: *Central Indiana Podiatry, P.C. v. Krueger* (Ind. 2008), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1134633.html]
- Geography must track where the employee worked: *Krueger* enforced only the three counties where the podiatrist practiced in his last two years. [cite: *Krueger*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1134633.html]
- Activity limits reaching work the employee never did are overbroad: "in any capacity" (*Med-1*); every customer and all competitive services (*Clark's Sales*). [cite: *Med-1 Solutions, LLC v. Taylor* (Ind. Ct. App. 2024), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html; *Clark's Sales & Service, Inc. v. Smith* (Ind. Ct. App. 2014), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/1658360.html]
- General skills and knowledge are not protectable; a continental-US scope failed. *Buffkin* did not rule on its 3-year term. [cite: *Buffkin v. Glacier Group* (Ind. Ct. App. 2013), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/1646418.html]

### Blue pencil (eraser only)
- Courts may strike unreasonable, divisible language but may not add, change or rearrange terms; a reformation clause doesn't expand that power. Applies to all restrictive covenants in the agreement. [cite: *Heraeus*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html]
- If striking can't leave a reasonable restriction, the whole covenant fails (*Clark's Sales*: "an indiscrete whole"); a severability clause asking the court to reduce the restraint is "inoperative" (*Med-1*). [cite: *Clark's Sales*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/1658360.html; *Med-1*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]

### Consideration
- Continued at-will employment is valid consideration, even for a covenant signed years in under "sign or be fired." [cite: *Med-1* (quoting *Ackerman v. Kimball Int'l*, not opened), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]

### Non-solicits, no-hire and NDAs
- An employee non-solicit covering any employee is overbroad unless limited to employees whose knowledge would give a competitor an unfair advantage; if deletion can't cure it, it is void. [cite: *Heraeus*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html]
- Customer non-solicits are restrictive covenants subject to the same blue pencil. [cite: *Heraeus*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html]
- "Prospective customer" clauses are likely overbroad. [UNVERIFIED: secondary source only (Ogletree 2020)] [cite: Ogletree, https://ogletree.com/insights/2020-01-23/indiana-supreme-court-reaffirms-that-the-blue-pencil-doctrine-is-an-eraser-for-overbroad-restrictive-covenant-language]
- On a preliminary-injunction record, file-access logs without proof of taking or use fell short. Inevitable disclosure is recognized only "on extreme facts." [cite: *Med-1*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]

### Remedies
- Preliminary injunction: likelihood of success, inadequate legal remedy, balance of harms, public interest. Hard-to-quantify lost patients and referrals can show an inadequate legal remedy. [cite: *Krueger*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1134633.html; *Med-1*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]
- Tolling: no verified Indiana authority on contractual tolling clauses or court-ordered extension (see Open questions). [cite: research/02-restrictive-covenants.md, I19]

### Reasonableness test
- Five parts: (1) necessary to protect a legitimate interest, (2) reasonably limited in time and place, (3) not unduly harsh on livelihood, (4) reasonable under public policy, (5) supported by valuable consideration. Disfavored and strictly construed against the employer. [cite: *Rental Uniform Serv. v. Dudley*, 278 S.C. 674 (1983), as quoted in *Fay v. Total Quality Logistics*, Op. 5471, pg. 7, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf]
- Geography is generally reasonable if confined to territory where the employee had customer contact. [cite: *Team IA, Inc. v. Lucas*, Op. 4889 (S.C. Ct. App. 2011), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4889.htm]
- No geographic limit = void, and a court cannot add one. [cite: *Stonhard, Inc. v. Carolina Flooring Specialists*, Op. 26047 (S.C. 2005), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26047.htm]
- Time limits of 2–3 years have been approved in employment cases. [cite: *Fay*, pg. 7, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf]
- Sale-of-business covenants get more relaxed scrutiny; a 150-mile restriction was upheld. [cite: *Palmetto Mortuary Transport v. Knight Systems*, Op. 27833 (S.C. 2018), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27833.pdf]

### No blue pencil
- Restrictions "must stand or fall on their own terms"; a trial court's swap of 15 miles for 75 was reversed. [cite: *Poynter Investments v. Century Builders*, Op. 26821 (S.C. 2010), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26821.htm]
- Courts won't import a time limit from another paragraph. [cite: *Fay*, pg. 6, 9, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf]
- **Step-downs (Court of Appeals only):** an overbroad nationwide tier failed, but an alternative four-state tier could stand on the facts (remanded). The Supreme Court in *Poynter* didn't rule on the tiers. [cite: *Team IA*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4889.htm]
- **Choice of law:** SC honors the clause but won't apply a foreign state's reformation that adds terms the parties never agreed to; the *Fay* majority and concurrence split on method. [cite: *Stonhard*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26047.htm; *Fay*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf]

### Consideration
- At-hire covenant: employment itself is consideration. Mid-employment: needs "separate consideration, in addition to continued at-will employment"; unchanged duties, position and pay failed. A raise, promotion or new duties sufficed in an earlier case. [cite: *Poole v. Incentives Unlimited*, Op. 25299 (S.C. 2001), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/htmlfiles/SC/25299.htm]

### Non-solicits, no-hire and NDAs
- Customer non-solicits get the same test; territory ties to customer contact. [cite: *Team IA*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4889.htm]
- No verified SC appellate holding on employee non-solicits or no-hire clauses (see Open questions). [cite: research/02-restrictive-covenants.md, S13]
- NDAs and invention-assignment clauses are not restraints of trade and are not strictly construed, but must be reasonable. No time limit is not automatically fatal; holdover invention clauses need a reasonable time limit. NDAs broad enough to act as non-competes face the higher burden (n.4). [cite: *Milliken & Co. v. Morin*, Op. 27154 (S.C. 2012), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27154.pdf]
- An NDA covering nearly all information "at all times," with an inevitable-disclosure clause, was a disguised non-compete with no time limit: void, and § 39-8-30(D) didn't save it. [cite: *Fay*, pg. 6–9, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf]
- A trade-secret injunction can issue against an at-will employee with no covenant at all. [cite: *Jennings-Dill, Inc. v. Israel*, Op. 6046 (S.C. Ct. App. 2024), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/6046.pdf]

### Tolling and remedies
- Courts won't extend a covenant past its stated end as a remedy. [cite: *Stonhard*, question III, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26047.htm; *Poynter* (citing *MailSource*), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26821.htm]
- Contractual tolling clauses: no SC ruling found. [UNVERIFIED: not decided in any opinion opened] [cite: research/02-restrictive-covenants.md, S17]
- Preliminary injunction: irreparable harm, likelihood of success, no adequate remedy at law; no separate balancing step. [cite: *Poynter*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26821.htm]
- No damages under a covenant void when breached. [cite: *Stonhard*, question II, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26047.htm]
- Damages are available for breach of a valid covenant at least in sale-of-business cases: *Palmetto Mortuary* reinstated an injunction, fees and $373,264.54 in lost profits. [cite: *Palmetto Mortuary*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27833.pdf]
- No 2025–26 SC appellate decision on employment covenants found (index currency not guaranteed). [cite: sccourts.org opinion search, https://www.sccourts.org/opinions-orders/opinions/opinion-search/?type=P&keyword=covenant+not+to+compete]

## Facts that decide outcomes
- Employer side:
  - When it was signed, and what came with it. SC mid-employment needs new consideration (*Poole*); Indiana accepts continued employment (*Med-1*). [cite: *Poole*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/htmlfiles/SC/25299.htm; *Med-1*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]
  - The employee's real footprint: counties or territory worked, customers handled, confidential information accessed. [cite: *Krueger*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1134633.html; *Team IA*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4889.htm]
  - Exact covenant text: divisible clauses (Indiana eraser), a time and geographic limit, alternative tiers, choice-of-law clause. [cite: *Heraeus*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html; *Stonhard*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26047.htm]
  - Indiana physicians: original signing date, whether the employer is a hospital, parent, affiliated manager or hospital system, primary care status, the five § 2(a) terms. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
  - NDA scope and DTSA notice: limited definition, no inevitable-disclosure clause, immunity notice given. [cite: *Fay*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf; 18 U.S.C. § 1833, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
  - Forensic proof of taking or use, not just file access. [cite: *Med-1*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]
  - Federal exposure: blanket non-competes on rank-and-file or healthcare staff match the FTC's targets. [cite: FTC Rollins release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/ftc-takes-action-against-noncompete-agreements-securing-protections-workers]
- Employee side:
  - SC mid-employment signing with nothing new in return: the covenant likely fails. [cite: *Poole*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/htmlfiles/SC/25299.htm]
  - Overbreadth ("any capacity," all customers, nationwide, no time limit): Indiana won't add terms; SC won't rewrite. [cite: *Heraeus*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html; *Poynter*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26821.htm]
  - How it ended: Indiana physician fired without cause or quitting for cause. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2(b) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
  - Role: physician, primary care, hospital employer, or purely executive work (§ 1.7(b)). [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.7 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
  - Severance confidentiality or non-disparagement for NLRA-covered non-supervisors: unlawful if overbroad while *McLaren Macomb* stands. [cite: NLRB weekly summary, https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-april-6-10-2026]
  - Whether the employer can show anything beyond general skill. [cite: *Buffkin*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/1646418.html]

## Common offer-letter / situation risks and typical recommendations
- **Indiana hospital physician non-compete signed on or after 2025-07-01:** likely High (void) when the employer is a hospital, parent, affiliated manager or hospital system; typical fix: drop it, rely on an NDA and a ≤1-year employee non-solicit. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2.3, -1.4(b) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Hospital stay-or-pay repayment for a physician employed 3+ years:** likely High when triggered by leaving to practice elsewhere; typical fix: remove or limit repayment to under-3-year tenure. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.4(a)(2) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Indiana physician non-compete missing a § 2(a) term:** likely High (unenforceable) when any of the five terms is absent; typical fix: add all five, including the buyout option. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Primary care physician non-compete (Indiana, from 2023-07-01):** likely High; typical fix: delete it. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2.5 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **"Any capacity" or all-customer scope:** likely High in Indiana when the employee did narrower work; typical fix: limit to the role, customers handled and territory worked, in divisible clauses. [cite: *Med-1*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html; *Clark's Sales*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/1658360.html]
- **Relying on a reformation or severability clause:** likely High in both states; typical fix: draft each restriction to stand on its own. [cite: *Heraeus*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html; *Poynter*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26821.htm]
- **SC covenant with no geographic limit:** likely High (void); typical fix: territory tied to customer contact. [cite: *Stonhard*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26047.htm]
- **SC covenant added mid-employment with no new benefit:** likely High; typical fix: tie signing to a raise, promotion, bonus or new duties, documented. [cite: *Poole*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/htmlfiles/SC/25299.htm]
- **Employee non-solicit covering every employee:** likely Med–High in Indiana; typical fix: limit to employees whose knowledge would help a competitor. [cite: *Heraeus*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html]
- **NDA defining all information as confidential, forever, with an inevitable-disclosure clause:** likely High in SC; typical fix: define real categories, limit non-trade-secret information in time, drop the clause. [cite: *Fay*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf]
- **NDA with no DTSA immunity notice:** likely Med (lost exemplary damages and fees); typical fix: add the notice or a policy cross-reference. [cite: 18 U.S.C. § 1833, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- **NDA or severance clause that blocks SEC reports or harassment disclosures:** likely Med–High; typical fix: carve out regulator communications and Speak Out Act matters. [cite: 17 CFR 240.21F-17, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.21F-17; 42 U.S.C. § 19403, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/19403]
- **Broad severance non-disparagement for non-supervisors:** likely Med while *McLaren Macomb* stands; typical fix: narrow it and add an NLRA-rights savings clause. [cite: GC 26-04, https://apps.nlrb.gov/link/document.aspx/09031d4584351740]
- **Blanket non-competes for rank-and-file workers or healthcare staff:** likely Med federal enforcement risk; typical fix: narrow customer non-solicits instead. [cite: FTC Gateway release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-takes-action-protect-workers-noncompete-agreements; FTC healthcare warning letters, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-chairman-ferguson-issues-noncompete-warning-letters-healthcare-employers-staffing-companies]
- **B2B no-hire clause in a customer or vendor contract:** likely Med; typical fix: remove it or limit it to a narrow, justified window. [cite: FTC Adamas release, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-continues-enforcement-action-streak-against-anticompetitive-no-hire-agreements]
- **Indiana franchise covenant over 3 years or beyond the exclusive area:** likely High (unlawful); typical fix: cap at 3 years and the granted territory. [cite: IC 23-2-2.7-1(9) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/23]

## Creative angles (non-obvious, lawful)
- **Creative (employer, SC): draft separate, self-contained tiers** rather than relying on a court. Risk: the SC Supreme Court hasn't approved step-downs, and *Poynter* says "stand or fall." Enforceability: moderate, fact-dependent. [cite: *Team IA*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4889.htm]
- **Creative (employer, SC): pair a mid-employment covenant with real, documented new consideration.** Risk: nominal consideration may be challenged; the amount needed is unsettled. Enforceability: good if the benefit is real. [cite: *Poole*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/htmlfiles/SC/25299.htm]
- **Creative (employer): rely on trade-secret law instead of a non-compete.** SC § 39-8-30(B), the Indiana UTSA and the DTSA all allow injunctions for threatened misappropriation. Risk: must prove secrecy and misuse; DTSA injunctions can't block employment. Enforceability: good with forensic proof. [cite: *Jennings-Dill*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/6046.pdf; IC 24-2-3-3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- **Creative (employer): pay for the restraint (garden leave or paid restricted period).** It answers SC's "unduly harsh" prong and supplies separate consideration. Risk: no IN or SC case on garden leave; whether in-term paid restrictions draw less FTC attention is [UNVERIFIED: no source]. Enforceability: untested. [cite: *Fay* (five-part test), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf]
- **Creative (employee, Indiana physician): use the statutory buyout and mediation as leverage,** and argue a hospital's stay-or-pay repayment after 3 years is a void non-compete. Risk: only for covered dates and employers. Enforceability: strong where the statute applies. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2.6, -1.4(a)(2) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- **Creative (employee): attack an overbroad NDA as a disguised non-compete.** Risk: fails if the NDA is narrow; no Indiana authority found. Enforceability: good in SC. [cite: *Fay*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/5471.pdf; *Milliken* n.4, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27154.pdf]
- **Creative (employer): favor narrow customer non-solicits keyed to customers the employee served recently,** leaving general advertising and customer-initiated inquiries alone. Risk: state law still applies; this is an inference from two consent orders. [cite: Gateway final D&O, https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/gateway_decision_order.pdf; Rollins final D&O, https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/251_0011_rollins_do_public.pdf]
- **Creative (Indiana physician deal): structure as a bona fide sale of a majority-owned practice.** A sale-of-business covenant where the physician owns more than 50% is outside the hospital ban. Risk: must be a real sale, not a relabeled employment term. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.4(b)(3) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
