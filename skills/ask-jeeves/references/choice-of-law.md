# Choice of law and state selection: reference

> For issue-spotting only. Not legal advice. Every claim cites its source; [UNVERIFIED] marks claims that could not be confirmed.

How to use this file: Part 1 says how courts pick the governing state. Part 2 is the procedure the skill runs to propose a candidate state. Part 3 flags likely issues in any state. Part 4 lists the IN vs SC differences to show in the recommendation. Pins are slip-opinion pages ("slip pg. N") unless a reporter page is given.

## 1. Choice-of-law rules

### Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws
- **§ 187 (law the parties chose).** The chosen law applies unless (2)(a) the chosen state has "no substantial relationship to the parties or the transaction" and no other reasonable basis exists, or (2)(b) it is contrary to a fundamental policy of a state with a "materially greater interest" whose law would apply under § 188 absent the choice. Quoted in full in *Dearborn v. Everett J. Prescott, Inc.*, 486 F. Supp. 2d 802 (S.D. Ind. 2007), slip pg. 19. [cite: *Dearborn*, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-insd-1_07-cv-00078/pdf/USCOURTS-insd-1_07-cv-00078-0.pdf]
- § 187 cmt. g: a fundamental policy may be one protecting a party against "superior bargaining power"; employment covenants are the standard example (*Dearborn* slip pg. 20). [cite: *Dearborn*, same URL]
- **§ 188 (no effective choice).** The state with "the most significant relationship" under the § 6 principles governs. Contacts: (a) place of contracting, (b) place of negotiation, (c) place of performance, (d) location of the subject matter, (e) domicile, residence, nationality, place of incorporation and place of business. Quoted in *Aprio Advisory Grp., LLC v. Alexeev*, C.A. No. 2026-0508-JTL (Del. Ch. Oct. 16, 2026), slip pg. 12–13. [cite: *Aprio*, https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=402240]
- § 188 cmt. e: the place of performance "has an obvious interest" in whether performance is illegal, but carries "little weight" when performance is divided more or less equally among states. This is the remote-worker problem. *Hightower Holding, LLC v. Gibson*, C.A. 2022-0086-LWW (Del. Ch. Feb. 9, 2023), slip pg. 13 nn.52, 55 (contacts in n.49, slip pg. 12). [cite: *Hightower*, https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=343830]
- **§ 196 (contracts for services).** [UNVERIFIED: no opened opinion quotes its text; ALI text is paywalled. The usual reading is that the state where the services are mainly rendered governs absent a more significant relationship.] Use the verified § 188(2)(c) place-of-performance contact for the same practical point: where the employee works points to the default state. [cite: *Aprio*, slip pg. 12–13, https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=402240]
- **CHANGED 2025–26: Restatement (Third).** Not final; no official text. Tentative Draft 6 was approved "subject to the discussion" at the 2026 Annual Meeting and contains Ch. 8 (Contracts) §§ 8.01–8.14, incl. § 8.02 (choice-of-law clauses) and § 8.06 (service contracts). Ch. 8 first appeared in TD 4 (2023). The ALI says approved drafts may be cited as its position. [cite: ALI, https://www.ali.org/project/conflict-laws ; https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-third/conflict-laws-3d] Whether §§ 8.02/8.06 change § 187/§ 188 results: [UNVERIFIED: paywalled].

### How Indiana courts apply them
- **Clause favored.** Indiana doctrine "favors contractual stipulations as to governing law." *Allen v. Great Am. Reserve Ins. Co.*, 766 N.E.2d 1157, 1162 (Ind. 2002). The clause governed the contract claim only; tort and indemnity claims went to lex loci delicti. [cite: *Allen*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1153073.html]
- **No clause: "most intimate contacts."** Indiana weighs the same five contacts as § 188(2) (the opinion does not label them § 188). *Eby v. York-Division, Borg-Warner*, 455 N.E.2d 623 (Ind. Ct. App. 1983). [cite: *Eby*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/455/cases/0623-01.json]
- Indiana's own conflicts rules control in Indiana suits (Indiana Commercial Court Treatise, 2025 ed., pg. 28, citing *Hubbard Mfg. v. Greeson*, 515 N.E.2d 1071 (Ind. 1987)). Do **not** cite *Hubbard* for the contract test or for honoring clauses: it is a wrongful-death tort case. [cite: Treatise, https://www.in.gov/courts/iocs/files/commercial-court-treatise.pdf]
- **Out-of-state clause refused for an overbroad non-compete.** *Dearborn* (S.D. Ind. 2007), slip pg. 17–32: the Maine clause would have let a court narrow the covenant; Indiana will not rewrite one, so the court refused Maine law under § 187(2)(b). It used the *Trimble v. Ameritech*, 700 N.E.2d 1128 (Ind. 1998) public-policy factors (slip pg. 22–23) and IC 24-1-2-1, and found Indiana's interest materially greater (slip pg. 30). PI denied. [cite: *Dearborn*, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-insd-1_07-cv-00078/pdf/USCOURTS-insd-1_07-cv-00078-0.pdf]
  - Slip pg. 17: Indiana enforces such clauses where bargaining power is comparable, "(unlike this case)." [cite: same]
  - Earlier refusals, verified only as quoted in *Dearborn*: *South Bend Consumers Club v. United Consumers Club*, 572 F. Supp. 209 (N.D. Ind. 1983) (Illinois clause, franchise non-compete; slip pg. 20); *Wright-Moore Corp. v. Ricoh Corp.*, 908 F.2d 128 (7th Cir. 1990) (New York clause; slip pg. 18, 21). [cite: same]
  - *Dearborn* is a federal prediction. No Indiana state appellate case refusing a non-compete choice-of-law clause was found.
- IC 24-1-2-1 (restraint-of-trade statute relied on in *Dearborn*) is in force; it makes contracts "in restraint of trade or commerce" illegal. [cite: IC 24-1-2-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- **No rewriting, so the choice matters.** The blue pencil "is really an eraser": courts may strike divisible terms but not add terms. *Heraeus Med., LLC v. Zimmer, Inc.* (Ind. Dec. 3, 2019) (135 N.E.3d 150 is secondary). [cite: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2034943.html ; https://caseclips.courts.in.gov/2019/12/09/heraeus-v-zimmer]
- Physician non-competes are not per se void at common law. *Central Ind. Podiatry v. Krueger*, 882 N.E.2d 723 (Ind. 2008). [cite: https://static.case.law/ne2d/882/cases/0723-01.json]
- **Forum clauses.** Enforced if freely negotiated and reasonable and just; the resisting party "faces a steep climb." *Perdue Farms, Inc. v. L&B Transp., LLC*, No. 24S-PL-40 (Ind. Aug. 13, 2024), slip pg. 5 (239 N.E.3d 842 is secondary). The clause bound the contracting party, not its non-party employees. [cite: Treatise pg. 117–118, https://www.in.gov/courts/iocs/files/commercial-court-treatise.pdf]
  - A valid forum clause does not defeat personal jurisdiction. *O'Bryant v. Adams*, 123 N.E.3d 689 (Ind. 2019). [cite: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/2002726.html]
  - A clickwrap forum clause was enforced as "reasonable and just." *Adsit Co. v. Gustin*, 874 N.E.2d 1018 (Ind. Ct. App. 2007). [cite: https://static.case.law/ne2d/874/cases/1018-01.json]
- **Indiana statutes: no anti-waiver or choice-of-law override.** IC 25-22.5-5.5 (physician non-competes) has no choice-of-law or anti-waiver clause; § 4 only preserves other terms and remedies. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-4 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
  - The chapter applies to physician non-competes originally entered into on/after July 1, 2020. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
  - **CHANGED 2025–26:** § 2.3: "Notwithstanding any other law," a physician and a hospital, a hospital's parent company, an affiliated manager, or a hospital system may not enter into a noncompete on/after July 1, 2025; violations are "void and unenforceable." Agreements originally entered into before July 1, 2025 are exempt. Added by P.L.207-2025 (SEA 475). [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2.3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25] *Correction: the research marked "P.L. 207-2025" UNVERIFIED; the 2026 official code confirms it. The 2025-05-06 signing date stays secondary.*
  - "Originally entered into" means the first-time date, not an amendment or renewal. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.6 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
  - "Noncompete agreement" in § 2.3 is broad: it reaches repayment penalties after 3+ years' employment, consent requirements and indirect restraints; it excludes NDAs, 1-year employee non-solicits and >50%-owner sale agreements. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.4 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
  - § 2.5 (P.L.165-2023): primary-care physicians and employers may not enter into a noncompete; § 2.5(b) opens "Notwithstanding any other law" (agreements before July 1, 2023 exempt). [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-2.5 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25] The "notwithstanding" language supports a § 187(2)(b) fundamental-policy argument (inference, not a holding).
  - No 2026 amendment: every section history in IC 25-22.5-5.5 ends at P.L.207-2025 or earlier in the 2026 edition. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25] Other 2026 session bills (SB 132, HB 1054) died, secondary only.
- **Trade secrets.** Indiana Uniform Trade Secrets Act, IC 24-2-3. [cite: IC 24-2-3-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24] *Correction: the research had this as treatise-only (secondary); the official code confirms it.*

### How South Carolina courts apply them
- **Chosen law honored, tested against SC public policy.** Non-compete terms "may be construed according to the law of another state," but SC won't enforce a result contrary to its public policy; adding an unnegotiated geographic limit violates it. *Stonhard, Inc. v. Carolina Flooring Specialists, Inc.*, 366 S.C. 156, 621 S.E.2d 352 (2005) (New Jersey clause, certified question). [cite: https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26047.htm]
- An Ohio clause did not save an NDA that worked as a perpetual non-compete; the majority "must also assess" SC public policy (adv. sheet pg. 23) and found an added term would fail in SC even if Ohio allowed it (n.2, pg. 25–26). *Fay v. Total Quality Logistics, LLC*, 419 S.C. 622, 799 S.E.2d 318 (Ct. App. 2017). [cite: https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/advSheets/no92017.pdf]
  - The *Fay* concurrence (pg. 27–31) quotes *Team IA, Inc. v. Lucas*, 395 S.C. 237, 248 (Ct. App. 2011): clauses "are generally honored in South Carolina" (pg. 28); and, through *Team IA*, *Nucor Corp. v. Bell*, 482 F. Supp. 2d 714, 728 (D.S.C. 2007): a clause violating SC policy is not enforced (pg. 29). Both verified only as quoted. [cite: same]
- **No blue pencil.** Restrictions "cannot be rewritten by a court"; blue-penciling the territory was error (business-sale agreement). *Poynter Invs. v. Century Builders of Piedmont*, 387 S.C. 583, 694 S.E.2d 15 (2010). [cite: https://static.case.law/sc/387/cases/0583-01.json]
- **No clause: lex loci contractus.** The law of the place the contract was made governs validity and interpretation; the place of performance may govern performance. *Lister v. NationsBank of Del.*, 329 S.C. 133, 494 S.E.2d 449 (Ct. App. 1997). It applied the most-significant-relationship test only "assumptively." Treat the SC default as unsettled for remote work. [cite: https://static.case.law/sc/329/cases/0133-01.json]
- **Forum statute, S.C. Code § 15-7-120.** (A): despite a clause requiring suit elsewhere, an action "alternatively may be brought" in SC. (B): an out-of-state arbitration-location clause is unenforceable for claims otherwise triable in SC. Applies to contracts generally; it adds a venue, it does not void the clause. [cite: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c007.php]
  - Federal courts may still transfer under 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a); *T.R. Helicopters, LLC v. Bell Helicopter Textron, Inc.*, C/A No. 3:10-2250-JFA (D.S.C. Nov. 17, 2010), found no strong SC policy against forum clauses and transferred. [cite: secondary copy, https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/t-r-helicopters-llc-888806845]
- S.C. Code § 36-1-301 (UCC "reasonable relation") is limited to UCC transactions, so it does not govern employment covenants (reading of § 36-1-102). [cite: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t36c001.php]
- **Trade secrets.** SC Trade Secrets Act, § 39-8-10 et seq. Under § 39-8-30(D) a trade-secret NDA is not void for lacking time or geography limits, but *Fay* limits that. [cite: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- **2025–26: nothing enacted.** H.4767 (physician ban) passed the House 58–53 on 2026-03-26; Senate committee reported it 2026-05-05; no floor vote. S.46 and H.3273 died in committee. [cite: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess126_2025-2026/bills/4767.htm]

### Forum clauses (federal overlay)
- A forum clause is "prima facie valid" unless shown unreasonable (at 10), and fails if it contravenes the forum's strong public policy (at 15). *The Bremen v. Zapata Off-Shore Co.*, 407 U.S. 1 (1972). [cite: https://static.case.law/us/407/html/0001-01.html]
- *Atlantic Marine Constr. Co. v. U.S. Dist. Ct.*, 571 U.S. 49 (2013): § 1404(a) enforces a clause naming another federal forum (at 59); the clause gets controlling weight except in exceptional cases (at 60, 63); the plaintiff's choice gets no weight (at 63); the transfer does not carry the original venue's choice-of-law rules (at 64); state or foreign forum goes through forum non conveniens (at 60). [cite: https://static.case.law/us/571/html/0049-01.html]
- A federal diversity court applies the forum state's conflicts rules. *Klaxon Co. v. Stentor Elec. Mfg. Co.*, 313 U.S. 487, 496 (1941). [cite: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/313/487]
- So a forum clause often decides the choice of law in practice: the forum applies its own conflicts rules and its own view of "fundamental policy." [cite: *Klaxon*, same URL; *Atlantic Marine* at 64, https://static.case.law/us/571/html/0049-01.html]
- **Race to the courthouse.** *Advanced Bionics Corp. v. Medtronic, Inc.*, 29 Cal.4th 697 (2002): the trial court erred in restraining the Minnesota action, on comity and restraint grounds; first-filed rule does not apply between sovereigns. It did not decide the § 16600 question. [cite: https://static.case.law/cal-4th/29/cases/0697-01.json]
- **DTSA** (18 U.S.C. § 1836) is federal wherever brought: 3-year limit; an injunction may not "prevent a person from entering into an employment relationship." [cite: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1836] It does not preempt state law (§ 1838). [cite: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1838]
- **CHANGED 2025–26: FTC rule gone.** *Ryan, LLC v. FTC*, 746 F. Supp. 3d 369 (N.D. Tex. 2024) set the rule aside; the FTC voted 3–1 on 2025-09-05 to dismiss its appeals and accede to vacatur; 91 FR 6507 removed 16 CFR part 910 effective 2026-02-12. [cite: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule ; https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/02/12/2026-02866.txt]

### Clauses vs employee-protective policy: key cases
- California law can govern a Maryland employer's covenant with a non-resident recruited by a California employer (§ 187(2); CA interest materially greater). *Application Group, Inc. v. Hunter Group, Inc.*, 61 Cal.App.4th 881 (1998). [cite: https://static.case.law/cal-app-4th/61/cases/0881-01.json]
- Delaware clause refused; California's specific policy outweighs Delaware's general freedom of contract (slip pg. 12–13). *Ascension Ins. Holdings, LLC v. Underwood* (Del. Ch. Jan. 28, 2015). [cite: https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=218380]
- § 925(e) turns on actual individual representation: 2018 assumed counsel and applied Delaware law; 2019 found none and applied California law, voiding the non-compete (bench ruling recounted at slip pg. 5) and non-solicits (slip pg. 15–16). *NuVasive, Inc. v. Miles* (Del. Ch. 2018, 2019). [cite: https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=279200 ; https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=294330]
- Despite a Delaware clause in a sale-related agreement, Alabama law applied (all § 188 contacts in Alabama); PI denied. *Hightower* (Del. Ch. 2023). [cite: https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=343830]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** A California resident's Delaware forum and law clauses, signed in a PE deal, were voidable under § 925: the equity "partner" was functionally an employee (slip pg. 18–23); signing was a condition of employment (slip pg. 24); § 925(e) needs individual representation and negotiation, and deal counsel answering partners' questions did not count (slip pg. 24–25). Dismissed for lack of personal jurisdiction (slip pg. 29). *Aprio* (Del. Ch. Oct. 16, 2026). [cite: https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=402240]
- A Delaware clause no longer guarantees enforcement: no blue-penciling of facially unreasonable covenants. *Sunder Energy v. Jackson* (Del. Dec. 10, 2024); see also *Kodiak* (2022), *Payscale* (2025). [cite: https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=372810 ; https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=338810 ; https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=380760]

### Remote workers and multi-state facts
- **Moving to a protective state after leaving may not bring its law along.** Employee lived in NJ, worked mainly from NJ and NY for a MA employer, visited MA "at least 25 times," then moved to Los Angeles. Under MA's *Oxford* test (tracks § 187(2)), California lacked a materially greater interest despite § 16600.5; MA-law injunction affirmed. *DraftKings Inc. v. Hermalyn*, No. 24-1443 (1st Cir. Sept. 26, 2024) (reporter cite UNVERIFIED). [cite: https://www.ca1.uscourts.gov/sites/ca1/files/opnfiles/24-1443P-01A.pdf]
- **Statutory location tests differ; map each separately:** CA, MN reside AND work; MA reside OR work, 30 days before leaving; CO forum OR, law AND, at termination; WA "Washington-based"; DC more than 50% of work time in DC (§ 32-581.01); FL "primary place of work"; LA none. [cite: statute URLs in the override table; DC https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/32-581.01]
- Split work: place of performance carries little weight, so domicile and place of contracting gain; a domicile at signing suggested where non-competition would be performed (*Hightower* n.55). [cite: https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=343830]
- Trade-secret tort claims may follow where events happened, not the clause (*Allen*, Indiana). [cite: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1153073.html] A case holding UTSA claims follow Restatement § 145 tort rules: [UNVERIFIED: none found].

## 2. Decision procedure (primary and backup state)

Run this before any research. Mode changes only whose contacts are "ours"; the steps and questions stay the same. [cite: issue 09 Answer; research/04 §A9 with verifier edits]

### Step 0: gather the facts
- **Pre-fill from the input.** Read the pasted document or file first. Pull out any choice-of-law clause, forum clause, signing date, work location, occupation and pay; show them as pre-filled answers to correct. Ask only what's missing.
- **Ask in at most two AskUserQuestion rounds**, short options plus "Other" to type a state:
  - Round 1: Where does the employee work? (IN / SC / Remote from another state / Other; multi-select with time share if split.) Where do they live? (Same as work / IN / SC / Other.) Where is the employer based, and where does it operate? (IN / SC / Multi-state / Other.) Where did the key events happen: resignation, data access, competing work? (Multi-select, or "Not yet".)
  - Round 2, only if still unknown: Does the contract name a governing-law state or a court? (Yes, which? / No / No contract.) Occupation and pay band (healthcare? broadcast?). Signing date.

### Steps
1. **Collect facts.** Residence now and at signing; work state(s) and share of time in each; employer HQ; choice-of-law and forum clauses; where signed and negotiated; signing, renewal and termination dates; occupation (healthcare, broadcast, construction) and pay; whether the employee had individual counsel; any suit filed or threatened, and where; claim type (contract covenant, trade secret, or both).
2. **Apply override statutes first.** If the employee meets an override state's location test (override table; location tests above), that state is **primary** for the covenant issue whatever the clause says. If two tests are met (e.g., lives in MA, works in CO), make one primary, the other backup, and flag the conflict. Minnesota's override reaches only claims under § 181.988.
3. **Florida check.** If the primary place of work is Florida, or the employer is Florida-headquartered and the clause picks Florida law, and pay exceeds 2x the annual mean wage of the employer's principal-place-of-business county (employee's residence county if that place is outside FL), the CHOICE Act may apply regardless of the clause. Healthcare practitioners are excluded. Treat Florida as primary in employer mode and a serious backup in employee mode.
4. **Otherwise start from the clause.** If a clause exists and the chosen state has a substantial relationship (§ 187(2)(a)), the chosen state is the provisional primary.
5. **Run the § 187(2)(b) test.** Find the default state under § 188 (usually where the employee mainly works; where they live if work is split). Ask: does the default state's law void or narrow the covenant where the chosen law would enforce or reform it? Is that a fundamental policy (a statute, a no-reformation rule, an occupation ban)?
   - Yes: **default state primary, chosen state backup.**
   - No: chosen state primary, default state backup.
6. **Account for the forum.** If a forum clause or pending suit puts the case in a court applying its own conflicts rules (Delaware, Massachusetts, or a federal court under *Klaxon*), note that the forum's approach may decide steps 4–5. Name the likely forum next to the primary state.
7. **No clause.** Primary is the § 188 default (place of performance, then domicile). Backup is the next strongest contacts, usually the employer's HQ. If the forum is South Carolina and the signing state differs from the work state, SC may apply lex loci contractus (*Lister*): name the signing state as backup and flag it.
8. **Split claims.** For trade-secret tort claims, add a line naming the state where the misappropriation happened, plus the DTSA. The contract clause may not govern them.
9. **Effective dates.** Check signing and renewal dates against the Part 3 change dates before applying a new statute. Do not assume every new law is prospective only; some reach existing agreements or pending proceedings:
   - WA ESHB 1155 from 2027-06-30, "regardless of when" entered;
   - OR HB 3410, "before, on or after" SB 951;
   - LA Act 273, also applies to existing contracts;
   - TN HB 1034, applies to "proceedings occurring" on/after 2026-07-01.
   - Indiana § 2.3 runs from the date "originally entered into"; an amendment or renewal does not restart it (IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.6).
10. **State the confidence and get confirmation.** Say **Uncertain** whenever any signal in the checklist below is present. Name primary and backup with one-line reasons, then confirm before research starts.

### Uncertainty checklist (any one = "Uncertain")
- [ ] The clause names a state where the employee neither lives nor works (often Delaware or the employer's HQ state).
- [ ] Lives in one state and works in another, works remotely for an out-of-state employer, or splits work time across states.
- [ ] Lives or works in an override state: CA, CO, LA, MA, MN, WA; NM, UT, VT for covered professions; FL in the employer-favoring direction.
- [ ] Moved since signing, or plans to move to a protective state (*DraftKings*).
- [ ] The forum clause names a different state from the choice-of-law clause, or a forum whose conflicts rules favor enforcement.
- [ ] Litigation is filed, or likely, in another state (race to the courthouse; *Advanced Bionics*).
- [ ] The chosen law would let a court rewrite a covenant the home state would void (IN eraser rule, *Heraeus*; SC no blue pencil, *Poynter*; WI § 103.465; NE *Waadah*).
- [ ] Signed in a sale-of-business or equity deal (*Hightower*, *Aprio*).
- [ ] Physician or other healthcare worker (occupation bans in more than a dozen states, Part 3).
- [ ] Signed, renewed or amended near an effective date: WA 2027-06-30; TN 2026-07-01; VA 2025-07-01 and 2026-07-01; WY 2025-07-01; FL 2025-07-01; IN 2025-07-01; ME c.718 (2026-07-29).
- [ ] Counsel was involved in signing (CA § 925(e); *NuVasive*, *Aprio*).
- [ ] The claim is a trade-secret tort, not a contract claim; the clause may not reach it (*Allen*).

## Facts that decide outcomes
- Employer side: a clause naming a state with real contacts (§ 187(2)(a)); comparable bargaining power or individual counsel (*Dearborn* slip pg. 17; § 925(e)); a narrow, divisible covenant that survives the eraser or no-blue-pencil rule; a forum clause in a court favoring enforcement (*Atlantic Marine*); signing before an effective date. [cite: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-insd-1_07-cv-00078/pdf/USCOURTS-insd-1_07-cv-00078-0.pdf ; https://static.case.law/us/571/html/0049-01.html]
- Employee side: living and working in an override state; a take-it-or-leave-it form; a covenant that only survives by rewriting; healthcare occupation; split work that weakens the employer's place-of-performance contact. [cite: override table URLs; https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=402240]

## Common situation risks and typical recommendations
- Out-of-state clause (e.g., Delaware) with an IN or SC worker: likely **Med–High** that the home state's policy displaces it when the covenant needs rewriting; typical fix: redraft narrowly under the home state's law. [cite: *Dearborn*; *Stonhard*, URLs above]
- Indiana physician with a hospital or hospital-system employer, originally signed on/after 2025-07-01: likely **High** the non-compete is void; typical fix: rely on NDA and a 1-year employee non-solicit, which § 1.4(b) excludes. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.4, -2.3, https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- SC forum clause naming another state: likely **Med** the suit can still be filed in SC (§ 15-7-120), but federal transfer remains possible. [cite: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c007.php]
- Remote worker who moved to a protective state after leaving: likely **Med** the old state's law still applies (*DraftKings*). [cite: https://www.ca1.uscourts.gov/sites/ca1/files/opnfiles/24-1443P-01A.pdf]
- Trade-secret claim: likely **Med** the clause doesn't govern; plead state UTSA and DTSA. [cite: *Allen*; https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1836]

## Creative angles (non-obvious, lawful)
- Employee mode in SC: file first in SC under § 15-7-120 so SC courts apply SC public policy. Risk: § 1404(a) transfer in federal court (*T.R. Helicopters*, secondary copy). Enforceability: statute is clear for state court venue. [cite: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c007.php]
- Employer mode: a Delaware law clause may not help an individual employee agreement, because § 2708(c)(2) excludes contracts under $100,000 and Delaware courts refuse to blue-pencil (*Sunder*). Risk: wasted drafting. [cite: https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c027/sc01/index.html ; https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=372810]
- Employer mode, CA-connected hire: real individual counsel who negotiates the terms is what triggers § 925(e); deal counsel does not count (*Aprio*). Risk: the exception is narrow. [cite: https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=402240]
- Indiana physician renewals: an amendment or renewal of a pre-2025-07-01 agreement does not make it "originally entered into" later, so § 2.3 may not reach it. Risk: a fully new agreement would be covered. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.6, https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
