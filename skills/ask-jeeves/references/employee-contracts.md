# Employee contracts: reference

> For issue-spotting only. Not legal advice. Every claim cites its source; [UNVERIFIED] marks claims that could not be confirmed.

Scope: offer letters, at-will, IP and invention assignment, device, BYOD and return-of-property terms, wage deductions and clawbacks, arbitration and the EFAA, severance and releases. Non-competes, non-solicits and NDAs are covered in depth in `restrictive-covenants.md`; this file touches them only where an offer letter does.

## Federal

### At-will and discharge limits
- No federal statute makes employment at-will; state law sets the default. This is an inference from the absence of one. Title VII bars discharge because of race, color, religion, sex or national origin. [cite: 42 U.S.C. §2000e-2(a)(1), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/2000e-2]
- Title VII bars retaliation for opposing an unlawful practice, filing a charge or taking part in a proceeding. [cite: 42 U.S.C. §2000e-3(a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/2000e-3]

### IP and invention assignment
- "Work made for hire" = (1) a work an employee prepares within the scope of employment, or (2) a specially commissioned work in nine listed categories, only by a written instrument both parties sign. Contractor software usually falls outside the nine. [cite: 17 U.S.C. §101, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/101]
- For a work made for hire the employer is the author unless a signed writing says otherwise. [cite: 17 U.S.C. §201(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/201]
- A copyright transfer (other than by operation of law) needs a writing signed by the owner. [cite: 17 U.S.C. §204(a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/204]
- Patents are assignable by written instrument. An assignment not recorded at the USPTO within 3 months (or before a later purchase) is void against a later bona fide purchaser. [cite: 35 U.S.C. §261, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/261]
- *Stanford v. Roche*: Bayh-Dole doesn't vest title in federal contractors; invention rights start with the inventor. The Federal Circuit's "agree to assign" (future promise) vs. "do hereby assign" (present transfer) contrast appears in the Court's background and the dissent; fn. 2 says only the Court had no occasion to rule on it. The "hereby assign" rule still governs in the Federal Circuit. [cite: *Bd. of Trustees of Leland Stanford Jr. Univ. v. Roche Molecular Sys.*, 563 U.S. 776 (2011), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/09-1159.ZO.html]
- Who is an "employee" for work-for-hire turns on common-law agency, chiefly the right to control manner and means; skill, tools, location, duration, benefits and tax treatment also count. No single factor decides. [cite: *CCNV v. Reid*, 490 U.S. 730 (1989), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/490/730]

### Limits on confidentiality and non-disparagement terms
- DTSA immunity: confidential disclosure to a government official or attorney solely to report a suspected violation of law, or in a sealed court filing. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(1)–(2), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- Any agreement governing trade secrets or confidential information, entered into or updated after May 11, 2016, must give notice of that immunity. A cross-reference to a policy that states it is enough. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3)(A), (B), (D), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- No notice: the employer can't recover exemplary damages or attorney fees from that employee; other remedies remain. "Employee" includes contractors and consultants. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3)(C), (b)(4), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- Speak Out Act: a pre-dispute nondisclosure or nondisparagement clause is not judicially enforceable for a sexual-assault or sexual-harassment dispute (§19403(a)). Trade-secret protection survives (§19403(d)). Applies to claims filed on or after Dec. 7, 2022 (§19404). [cite: 42 U.S.C. §§19403–19404, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/19403]
- SEC Rule 21F-17(a): no one may impede an individual from communicating with the SEC, including by enforcing a confidentiality agreement. [cite: 17 C.F.R. §240.21F-17(a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.21F-17]
- EEOC rights can't be waived; a waiver can't stop anyone from filing a charge or joining an investigation. [cite: 29 U.S.C. §626(f)(4), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/626]

### NLRA limits (non-supervisory employees)
- *McLaren Macomb* (Feb. 21, 2023): merely offering a severance agreement with broad confidentiality or non-disparagement terms that waive §7 rights violates §8(a)(1). Correct cite is No. 58 (one 2026 Board summary misprints No. 48). [cite: *McLaren Macomb*, 372 NLRB No. 58, https://www.nlrb.gov/news-outreach/news-story/board-rules-that-employers-may-not-offer-severance-agreements-requiring]
- *Stericycle* (Aug. 2, 2023): a work rule is presumptively unlawful if it reasonably tends to chill §7 activity. [cite: *Stericycle, Inc.*, 372 NLRB No. 113, https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-july-31-august-4-0]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** GC 25-05 (Feb. 14, 2025) rescinded GC memos 23-05 (McLaren guidance), 23-08 and 25-01 (non-competes, stay-or-pay) and 23-02 (electronic monitoring). Memos are guidance; rescission doesn't overrule Board decisions. [cite: NLRB GC 25-05, https://www.nlrb.gov/news-outreach/news-story/gc-25-05-rescission-of-certain-general-counsel-memoranda]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** Board quorum restored Jan. 7, 2026; a fourth member was sworn in Aug. 17, 2026, giving a 3–1 Republican-appointed majority. [cite: NLRB Board roster, https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/who-we-are/the-board]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *Prime Communications* (Apr. 7, 2026): Board unanimously held overbroad severance confidentiality and non-disparagement terms unlawful; two members applied *McLaren* but said they were open to reconsidering it. [cite: 374 NLRB No. 88, https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-april-6-10-2026]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** GC 26-04 (Aug. 26, 2026): the General Counsel is arguing to overrule *McLaren* (in *Valley Radiology*) and *Stericycle* (in *Honeywell*); regions keep applying extant Board law meanwhile. [cite: NLRB GC 26-04, https://apps.nlrb.gov/link/document.aspx/09031d4584351740]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** Through the Oct. 5–9, 2026 summary, nothing overrules either case. *Beatrice Loving Heart* (Sept. 30, 2026) found overbroad exit-letter terms unlawful but severed a handbook confidentiality allegation "for further consideration," a sign *Stericycle* may be revisited. [cite: 375 NLRB No. 56, https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-september-28-1]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *Ralphs Grocery* (Aug. 10, 2026): banning disclosure of an arbitration's *existence* is unlawful; a majority upheld confidentiality of its content and outcome with an adequate savings clause (Board summary only). [cite: 375 NLRB No. 25, https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-august-10-14-2026]

### Wages, deductions, device charges, clawbacks (FLSA floor)
- Wages must be paid free and clear; a kick-back to the employer fails. Requiring employees to supply tools of the trade violates the Act in any week the cost cuts into minimum wage or overtime. [cite: 29 C.F.R. §531.35, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.35]
- In non-overtime weeks a deduction is allowed only if the employee still nets minimum wage in cash. [cite: 29 C.F.R. §531.36, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.36]
- Items primarily for the employer's benefit (tools of the trade) can't be credited as wages. [cite: 29 C.F.R. §531.3(d), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.3]
- DOL: deductions for shortages, damage to employer property and theft are for the employer's benefit and may not cut below minimum wage or overtime, even for negligence; requiring separate cash repayment doesn't avoid the rule. [cite: DOL WHD Fact Sheet #16, https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/16-flsa-wage-deductions]
- Exempt salary can't be reduced for quality or quantity of work; §541.602(b)'s permitted deductions don't cover lost, damaged or unreturned equipment. [cite: 29 C.F.R. §541.602, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/541.602]
- An improper-deduction practice can cost the exemption for the whole job classification under the same managers; safe harbor needs a communicated policy, complaint process and reimbursement. [cite: 29 C.F.R. §541.603, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/541.603]
- Training-cost repayment upheld as repayment of a voluntary loan, not a kick-back, where final-week pay stayed at or above minimum wage (following the 7th Circuit's *Heder v. City of Two Rivers*, 295 F.3d 777 (7th Cir. 2002)). [cite: *Gordon v. City of Oakland*, 627 F.3d 1092 (9th Cir. 2010), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-9th-circuit/1545305.html]
- SEC Rule 10D-1's mandatory clawback reaches only exchange-listed issuers; it gives a private employer no clawback right. [cite: 17 C.F.R. §240.10D-1, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.10D-1]

### Devices, BYOD, return of property
- CFAA "exceeds authorized access" = obtaining information the accesser isn't entitled to obtain. Civil suit needs a listed harm (usually $5,000+ loss in a year); 2-year limit. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1030(e)(6), (g), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1030]
- *Van Buren*: CFAA is a "gates-up-or-down inquiry"; misusing data you may reach (e.g., breach of a business-purposes-only policy) isn't a violation. Fn. 8 leaves open whether contract or policy limits can be "gates." [cite: *Van Buren v. United States*, 593 U.S. 374 (2021), slip op. pg. 13 fn. 8, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf]
- Stored Communications Act bars intentional unauthorized access to a facility that obtains stored communications; exception for the provider (e.g., the employer's own e-mail system) or the user. Civil remedies: $1,000 minimum, punitive if willful, fees. Inference, not a verified case: the provider exception doesn't reach personal Gmail or iCloud on a BYOD phone. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §§2701, 2707, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2701]

### Arbitration and class waivers
- FAA §2: arbitration clauses are valid, irrevocable and enforceable, subject to general contract defenses and (since 2022) the EFAA. [cite: 9 U.S.C. §2, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/2]
- FAA §1 exempts employment contracts of seamen, railroad employees and other workers engaged in foreign or interstate commerce. [cite: 9 U.S.C. §1, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/1]
- Class and collective waivers in employment arbitration are enforceable; the NLRA doesn't override them. [cite: *Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis*, 584 U.S. 497 (2018), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/16-285]
- Airline cargo loader is §1-exempt (*Saxon*, 596 U.S. 450); a transportation worker needn't be in the transportation industry (*Bissonnette*, 601 U.S. 246); a court must stay, not dismiss, on a §3 request (*Spizzirri*, 601 U.S. 472). [cite: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/21-309; https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/23-51; https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/22-1218]
- Waiving the right to arbitrate needs no showing of prejudice. [cite: *Morgan v. Sundance, Inc.*, 596 U.S. 411 (2022), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/21-328]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *Flowers Foods v. Brock* (May 28, 2026, unanimous): a worker carrying goods on an intrastate leg of an interstate journey can be §1-exempt without crossing state lines. The Court rejected only the bright-line cross-state rule and reserved whether an independent company or taking title to goods defeats the exemption. [cite: *Flowers Foods, Inc. v. Brock*, 608 U.S. 380 (2026), syllabus pg. 380–81, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/608us2r37_i425.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *Jules v. Andre Balazs Properties* (May 14, 2026, unanimous): a federal court that stayed claims under §3 can confirm or vacate the award with no independent jurisdictional basis (employment-discrimination case). [cite: 608 U.S. 200 (2026), https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/608us1r31_8759.pdf]

### EFAA (sexual harassment and assault)
- At the election of the person alleging the dispute, no predispute arbitration agreement or joint-action waiver is enforceable for a case that "relates to" a sexual-harassment or sexual-assault dispute. A court, not an arbitrator, decides applicability, even with a delegation clause. [cite: 9 U.S.C. §402(a)–(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/402]
- Applies to disputes or claims that arise or accrue on or after Mar. 3, 2022; covers joint-action waivers inside or outside an arbitration clause. [cite: 9 U.S.C. §401; Pub. L. 117-90 §3, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/401]
- "Accrue" has its limitations meaning; a hostile-environment course of conduct continuing past Mar. 3, 2022 accrues after the Act. The test is claim accrual, not the agreement's date. [cite: *Olivieri v. Stifel, Nicolaus & Co.*, No. 23-658 (2d Cir. 2024), pg. 2, 5, 25, https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca2-23-00658/pdf/USCOURTS-ca2-23-00658-0.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** "Dispute arises" is a separate prong: it arises on adversity (opposition or disagreement). A pre-Act dispute took the case outside the EFAA. [cite: *Cornelius v. CVS Pharmacy Inc.*, No. 23-2961 (3d Cir. Apr. 2, 2025), pg. 10–12, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca3-23-02961/pdf/USCOURTS-ca3-23-02961-0.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** 6th Cir. adversity test: a dispute arises when the employee voices disagreement and the employer opposes it, expressly or constructively. [cite: *Memmer v. United Wholesale Mortgage, LLC*, No. 24-1144 (6th Cir. Apr. 18, 2025), pg. 10, 15, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca6-24-01144/pdf/USCOURTS-ca6-24-01144-0.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** One plausibly pleaded sexual-harassment claim takes the entire case (including ADA claims) out of arbitration; the court left open plausibility vs. "nonfrivolous" as the pleading test. [cite: *Bruce v. Adams & Reese, LLP*, 168 F.4th 367 (6th Cir. Feb. 25, 2026), pg. 2, 8, 15–17, https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/26a0054p-06.pdf] [UNVERIFIED: F.4th cite taken from a later opinion, reporter not opened]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** A plaintiff who discovers an EFAA-covered claim during arbitration may leave and take the entire case to court; starting arbitration on other claims was neither election nor waiver (rests on a finding she didn't then know of the claim). [cite: *Ding v. Structure Therapeutics, Inc.*, No. 25-1532 (9th Cir. Aug. 19, 2026), pg. 16, 20–21, https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/19/25-1532.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** A properly invoked EFAA election invalidates the arbitration agreement as to the entire lawsuit. [cite: *Diaz-Roa v. Hermes Law, P.C.*, No. 24-3223-cv (2d Cir. Sept. 30, 2026), pg. 2, https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/24-3223/24-3223-2026-09-30.pdf]
- 4th Cir. (SC): only an unpublished, non-binding ruling that the EFAA isn't retroactive to pre-Mar. 3, 2022 claims; no published scope decision found. [cite: *Bopda v. Comcast of the District, LLC*, No. 23-2148 (4th Cir. 2024) (unpub.), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca4-23-02148/pdf/USCOURTS-ca4-23-02148-0.pdf]
- 7th Cir. (IN): no EFAA opinion found on a govinfo full-text search. [UNVERIFIED: Indiana and South Carolina district courts not searched (rate limits)]

### Severance and releases
- OWBPA (age 40+): an ADEA waiver is knowing and voluntary only if it is written to be understood; names the ADEA; doesn't waive post-signing claims; gives consideration beyond what's owed; advises in writing to consult a lawyer; allows 21 days to consider (45 for a group/exit program) and 7 to revoke; and, for a group program, discloses the decisional unit, eligibility factors, and job titles and ages of those selected and not. [cite: 29 U.S.C. §626(f)(1)(A)–(H), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/626]
- EEOC regs: the 7-day revocation can't be shortened; a material change restarts the 21/45 days; nothing may bar or penalize filing a charge, including one challenging the waiver. [cite: 29 C.F.R. §1625.22(e), (i), https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-16/title-29.xml?part=1625&section=1625.22]
- No tender-back required before an ADEA suit; a covenant not to sue can't carry tender-back or fee-shifting penalties for an ADEA suit; setoff is capped. [cite: 29 C.F.R. §1625.23, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-16/title-29.xml?part=1625&section=1625.23]
- A release that fails OWBPA can't bar the ADEA claim; keeping the severance doesn't ratify it. [cite: *Oubre v. Entergy Operations, Inc.*, 522 U.S. 422 (1998), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/522/422]
- EEOC's 2009 severance Q&A is still posted: employees can still file charges after signing; courts split on tender-back for non-ADEA claims. [cite: https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/qa-understanding-waivers-discrimination-claims-employee-severance-agreements]
- FLSA rights can't be privately waived absent a bona fide dispute. [cite: *Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil*, 324 U.S. 697 (1945), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/324/697]
- Courts refuse to enforce wholly private FLSA settlements; the routes are DOL-supervised settlement (§216(c)) or court approval (7th and 11th Circuits). [cite: *Walton v. United Consumers Club, Inc.*, 786 F.2d 303, 306 (7th Cir. 1986), https://static.case.law/f2d/786/cases/0303-01.json; *Lynn's Food Stores, Inc. v. United States*, 679 F.2d 1350 (11th Cir. 1982), https://static.case.law/f2d/679/cases/1350-01.json]
- 4th Cir. has only FMLA dicta by analogy; district courts follow *Lynn's*. [cite: *Taylor v. Progress Energy*, 493 F.3d 454, 460 (4th Cir. 2007), https://static.case.law/f3d/493/cases/0454-01.json] [UNVERIFIED: no square 4th Cir. holding found]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** EEOC regained a quorum and voted 2–1 on Jan. 22, 2026 to rescind its 2024 harassment guidance; no 2025–26 vote touched releases, severance or arbitration. [cite: https://www.eeoc.gov/commission-votes-january-2026]

## Indiana

### At-will and how contracts alter it
- Two kinds of employment: definite or ascertainable term, or at-will; the at-will presumption is "strong." A definite-term contract ends early only for cause or by agreement unless an early-termination right was reserved. *Orr* held only that its handbook (suggestive discipline language plus a signed disclaimer) created no contract; it didn't decide whether a handbook can ever be one. [cite: *Orr v. Westminster Village North, Inc.*, 689 N.E.2d 712 (Ind. 1997), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1089282.html]
- Only three exceptions: adequate independent consideration, public policy, promissory estoppel. Quitting another job or moving, alone, is not adequate independent consideration. [cite: *Orr*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-supreme-court/1089282.html]
- Public policy covers discharge solely for exercising a statutory right (workers' comp claim) and for refusing an illegal act for which the employee would be personally liable. [cite: *Frampton v. Central Indiana Gas Co.*, 297 N.E.2d 425 (Ind. 1973), https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2031331/frampton-v-central-indiana-gas-company/; *McClanahan v. Remington Freight Lines*, 517 N.E.2d 390 (Ind. 1988), https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2097690/mcclanahan-v-remington-freight-lines-inc/]
- Leaving a "permanent" job on an assurance of permanent employment can be adequate independent consideration, enough to defeat summary judgment (no final finding of a contract). [cite: *Romack v. Public Service Co.*, 511 N.E.2d 1024 (Ind. 1987), https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2062078/romack-v-public-service-co-of-indiana/]
- Promissory estoppel on a job promise yields reliance damages only; lost post-discharge wages aren't recoverable. [cite: *Jarboe v. Landmark Community Newspapers*, 644 N.E.2d 118 (Ind. 1994), https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2150024/jarboe-v-landmark-community-newspapers-of-indiana-inc/]
- Continued at-will employment can support a restrictive covenant; a covenant barring work in any capacity was unreasonable and, with nothing to strike, unenforceable. [cite: *Med-1 Solutions, LLC v. Taylor*, No. 24A-PL-450 (Ind. Ct. App. Nov. 25, 2024), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html] [UNVERIFIED: whether published or a memorandum decision, which affects weight]

### IP and invention assignment
- No Indiana statute limits assignment of employee inventions (nothing like Cal. Lab. Code §2870). Re-checked 2026-10-20 by scanning all 37 titles of the 2026 Code for "invention," "inventor" and "intellectual property" near "employ": hits were only a patent-income tax provision (IC 6-3-2-21.7) and unrelated gaming-compact and higher-education disclosure text. Contract law governs scope. [cite: 2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition (all titles), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/6]
- No Indiana appellate decision on the reach of an invention-assignment clause was found. [UNVERIFIED: search budget ran out; prior research also found none]
- UTSA: trade secret = independent economic value from secrecy plus reasonable secrecy efforts (IC 24-2-3-2); the chapter displaces conflicting state law on misappropriation "except contract law and criminal law" (IC 24-2-3-1(c)); exemplary damages for willful and malicious misappropriation up to twice the subsection (a) award (IC 24-2-3-4(c)). No 2025–26 amendment. [cite: IC 24-2-3-1, -2, -4 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]

### Wage payment
- Pay at least semimonthly (biweekly if requested), for wages earned to not more than 10 business days before payday; any contract in violation of (a) is void. An employee who quits is paid on the next regular payday. [cite: IC 22-2-5-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Attorney fees and costs are mandatory in a wage suit; liquidated damages of 2x wages due apply only if the employer wasn't acting in good faith (P.L.193-2015; older 10%-per-day text quoted in *Steele* is superseded). [cite: IC 22-2-5-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Employer-separated employee: unpaid wages due on the regular payday for the pay period of separation. In a dispute the conceded amount must be paid "without condition," and accepting it is not a release of the balance. [cite: IC 22-2-9-2(a), -3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Which statute: employer-separated employees use the Wage Claims Statute (IC 22-2-9) via the Department of Labor; current employees and those who quit use the Wage Payment Statute (IC 22-2-5). [cite: *St. Vincent Hosp. & Health Care Ctr. v. Steele*, 766 N.E.2d 699 (Ind. 2002), https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/853188/st-vincent-hospital-health-care-center-inc-v-steele/]
- A bonus tied to production and overall business expenses is not a "wage"; a bonus for time worked can be. [cite: *Highhouse v. Midwest Orthopedic Inst.*, 807 N.E.2d 737 (Ind. 2004), https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/852921/highhouse-v-midwest-orthopedic-institute-pc/]
- Minimum wage tracks the federal FLSA rate for employers with at least 2 employees in a workweek; IC 22-2-2-4(c) was not amended in 2025–26. [cite: IC 22-2-2-4(c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** tip pooling added at IC 22-2-2-14.1 (P.L.15-2026); minimum-wage definitions in IC 22-2-2-3 amended by P.L.67-2025 (substance not examined). [cite: IC 22-2-2-3, -14.1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]

### Deductions and clawbacks (IC 22-2-6)

## South Carolina

### At-will and how contracts alter it
- A handbook or other document issued after June 30, 2004 creates no contract if conspicuously disclaimed. Handbook: disclaimer in underlined capital letters on the first page, signed by the employee. Other documents: underlined capitals on page 1, no signature. Conspicuousness is a question of law. [cite: S.C. Code §41-1-110, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c001.php]
- Pre-July 2004 handbooks: a disclaimer is "merely one factor" and contract existence is a jury question; mandatory handbook procedures can bind the employer (*Small*, as later opinions describe it). [cite: *Conner v. City of Forest Acres*, Op. No. 25961 (S.C. 2005), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/25961.htm]
- Discharge violating a clear mandate of public policy is a tort; applies to at-will contracts with a notice provision; a definite-term employee can't be discharged early without just cause. [cite: *Stiles v. American General Life Ins. Co.*, Op. No. 24945 (S.C. 1999), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/24945.htm]
- The exception isn't limited to illegal-act cases, but doesn't apply where a statutory wrongful-termination remedy exists; the Payment of Wages Act has none. [cite: *Barron v. Labor Finders of S.C.*, Op. No. 27018 (S.C. 2011), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/27018.htm]
- At-will status can be altered by an oral contract of definite employment; vague assurances ("keep your nose clean") are puffery. [cite: *Prescott v. Farmers Telephone Coop.*, Op. No. 24948 (S.C. 1999), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/24948.htm]
- An offer e-mail guaranteeing first-year salary and second-year earnings created a two-year term contract, defeating the at-will defense. [cite: *Mathis v. Brown & Brown of S.C., Inc.*, 698 S.E.2d 773 (S.C. 2010), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26851.htm]
- No 2025–26 SC appellate decision on at-will or wrongful discharge found (published opinions scanned). [cite: https://www.sccourts.org/]

### Wages, deductions, clawbacks (Payment of Wages Act; no amendment since 1993)
- "Wages" includes commissions, other methods, and vacation, holiday and sick pay due under policy or contract; severance was deleted from the definition in 1990. [cite: S.C. Code §41-10-10(2), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- At hire, written notice of wages, pay times and deductions; changes in writing at least 7 calendar days before effect (raises excepted). Employers with fewer than five employees throughout the prior 12 months, and domestic employers, are exempt from all of §41-10-30. [cite: S.C. Code §§41-10-20, -30(A), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- No withholding or diverting wages unless required or permitted by law or noticed in writing under §41-10-30(A). [cite: S.C. Code §41-10-40(C), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- Separated employee ("for any reason"): pay within 48 hours or by the next regular payday, not more than 30 days. [cite: S.C. Code §41-10-50, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- Dispute: pay the conceded amount unconditionally; accepting it isn't a release. [cite: S.C. Code §41-10-60, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- Recovery of three times unpaid wages plus costs and fees as the court allows; 3-year limit. [cite: S.C. Code §41-10-80(C), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- The chapter can't be contravened or set aside by private agreement. [cite: S.C. Code §41-10-100, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- Trebling is discretionary (case law, not the statute): unjust where there is a bona fide dispute, judged when wages were withheld. Post-termination earnings aren't "wages." [cite: *Mathis*, quoting *Rice v. Multimedia, Inc.*, 456 S.E.2d 381 (S.C. 1995), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26851.htm]
- **CHANGED 2025–26 (case law):** *Hess* affirmed recovery and trebling of contractually owed annual bonuses withheld without a good-faith basis (pg. 14–17), without separately deciding bonuses are "wages"; vague employer-drafted bonus terms read against the employer (pg. 10–11); discovery rule applies to the 3-year limit (pg. 8–9); a changed bonus formula didn't bind without the 7-day notice (pg. 12–14). [cite: *Hess v. Morphis Pediatric Group of Lancaster, P.A.*, Op. No. 6115 (S.C. Ct. App. July 9, 2025), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/6115.pdf]
- SC appellate law on deductions for unreturned property, devices or training costs, and on whether a negotiated release of a disputed wage claim survives §41-10-100. [UNVERIFIED: no case found; search budget ran out]

## Common offer-letter / situation risks and typical recommendations
- Guarantee language ("first-year salary," "two-year earnings," "permanent"): likely Med when the offer states pay or duration; typical fix: state at-will plainly, label figures as current rates, and in SC put the disclaimer in underlined caps on page 1. [cite: *Mathis*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26851.htm; *Romack*, https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2062078/romack-v-public-service-co-of-indiana/]
- IP assignment worded "agree to assign" or unsigned: likely Low for employer form when "hereby assign" and a signature are present, Med for employee scope (off-hours and post-employment work); typical fix: "hereby assign," signed writing, prior-inventions schedule, USPTO recording within 3 months. [cite: 17 U.S.C. §204(a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/204; 35 U.S.C. §261, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/261]
- Contractor deliverables treated as work for hire: likely Med when software falls outside the nine categories; typical fix: an express signed assignment. [cite: 17 U.S.C. §101, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/101]
- Confidentiality clause without DTSA notice or regulator carve-outs: likely Med; typical fix: DTSA notice (or policy cross-reference), SEC/EEOC carve-outs, Speak Out Act carve-out, §7 savings clause for non-supervisors. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833; *Prime Communications*, https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-april-6-10-2026]
- Final-paycheck setoff for an unreturned device: likely High in Indiana (no listed purpose; fine bar) and High in SC when not noticed in writing; typical fix: no payroll setoff; return-or-invoice clause and civil recovery. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b), IC 22-2-8-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22; S.C. Code §41-10-40(C), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- Device or equipment purchase by deduction (Indiana): likely Med when over vendor cost, over the $2,500/5% cap, or for OSHA protective gear; typical fix: vendor-cost pricing within the cap, revocable signed assignment. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b)(15), (d), (e) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Any deduction from an exempt employee's salary for equipment: likely High; risks the exemption for the class. [cite: 29 C.F.R. §§541.602–.603, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/541.603]
- Training, sign-on or bonus clawback from final pay: likely High when it cuts below the FLSA floor, when the Indiana assignment is revoked, or in SC without written notice; typical fix: separate pro-rated repayment agreement, actual cost, collected outside payroll. [cite: *Gordon*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-9th-circuit/1545305.html; IC 22-2-6-2(a)(1)(C) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Physician repayment tied to leaving (Indiana hospital systems, post-July 2025, 3+ years): likely High; can be a void noncompete. [cite: IC 25-22.5-5.5-1.4(a)(2) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/25]
- Arbitration by e-mail opt-out: likely High in SC; typical fix: affirmative signature or click. [cite: *Lampo*, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/28265.pdf]
- Arbitration for delivery or driver roles: likely Med after *Flowers Foods*; typical fix: state-law fallback (Indiana act covers employment). [cite: *Flowers Foods*, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/608us2r37_i425.pdf; IC 34-57-2-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34]
- Class waiver with harassment facts: likely High that the whole case leaves arbitration in the 2d, 6th and 9th Circuits. [cite: *Diaz-Roa*, https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/24-3223/24-3223-2026-09-30.pdf]
- Earned final pay conditioned on signing a release: likely High; conceded wages must be paid without condition. [cite: IC 22-2-9-3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22; S.C. Code §41-10-60, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- Release from a 40+ employee missing an OWBPA element, or with a shortened revocation period: likely High for the ADEA claim. [cite: *Oubre*, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/522/422; 29 C.F.R. §1625.22(e), https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-16/title-29.xml?part=1625&section=1625.22]
- Release covering FLSA claims without DOL or court approval: likely High (unenforceable). [cite: *Walton*, https://static.case.law/f2d/786/cases/0303-01.json]

## Creative angles (non-obvious, lawful)
- CREATIVE: Replace device deductions with a return-or-invoice clause plus a civil claim under IC 34-24-3-1 for criminal conversion (up to 3x damages and fees). Risk: must prove the IC 35-43-4-3 elements (knowing or intentional unauthorized control). Enforceability: Medium in Indiana; avoids the revocable-assignment and fine problems; no SC equivalent, since SC's computer-crime civil action needs a conviction. [cite: IC 34-24-3-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34; S.C. Code §16-16-25, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c016.php]
- CREATIVE: Draft training repayment as a separate pro-rated loan or promissory note at actual cost, never set off below the FLSA floor. In Indiana, (b)(7) lets a written-instrument employer loan be repaid by assignment (interest capped at prime + 4%), but the assignment stays revocable, so the note is the durable instrument. Risk: FTC focus on noncompete-linked "fees or penalties"; Indiana physician rule. Enforceability: Medium. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b)(7), (c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22; *Gordon*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-9th-circuit/1545305.html]
- CREATIVE: Offer the departing employee a written option to buy the device at vendor cost; in Indiana an employer-offered sale at the employee's written request is a listed purpose ((b)(6)). Risk: that a post-loss buyout fits (b)(6) is a reading of the text, not a verified holding; still revocable and subject to the FLSA floor. Enforceability: Low–Medium. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b)(6) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- CREATIVE: In SC, put the at-will disclaimer and arbitration notice both in underlined caps on page 1 of the offer letter, and state the FAA governs. Risk: that §41-1-110's "other document" covers an offer letter is a reading, not a holding. Enforceability: Low risk; the FAA statement avoids relying on the SC Act opt-in, which can't be mandatory. [cite: S.C. Code §§41-1-110, 15-48-10, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c048.php]
- CREATIVE: Give the DTSA notice once, by cross-reference to a policy that states the reporting procedure. Risk: Low. Enforceability: expressly allowed by statute. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3)(B), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- CREATIVE (employee): a plausible sexual-harassment claim can pull the whole case into court, even mid-arbitration. Risk: must survive the pleading test; open in the 4th and 7th Circuits. Enforceability: Medium. [cite: *Ding*, https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/19/25-1532.pdf]

Risk: High; no case found, a bona fide disputed-claim release may be upheld, and pressing it can cost the severance. [cite: S.C. Code §41-10-100, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]

Risk: Low legal risk; leverage is the obstacle. [cite: 2026 Ind. Code (all titles), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/6; https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/title41.php]
- CREATIVE: Treat NLRA confidentiality limits as live despite GC 25-05; add a §7 savings clause now and revisit if *McLaren* falls. Risk: Low. [cite: NLRB GC 26-04, https://apps.nlrb.gov/link/document.aspx/09031d4584351740]
