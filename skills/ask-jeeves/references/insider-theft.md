# Insider theft of IT assets, data and trade secrets: reference

> For issue-spotting only. Not legal advice. Every claim cites its source; [UNVERIFIED] marks claims that could not be confirmed.

Scope: an employee or other insider taking trade secrets, data or IT assets. Stolen devices and outside hacking are out of scope. `[INFERENCE]` marks reasoning from verified sources, not a holding. Case texts were read from the Caselaw Access Project mirror, which has no star pagination, so those cases carry no pin cites.

## Federal

### Defend Trade Secrets Act (civil), 18 U.S.C. §§1836, 1839
- Owner of a misappropriated trade secret may sue if the secret relates to a product or service used or intended for use in interstate or foreign commerce, §1836(b)(1). [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836 (2024 ed.), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Federal courts have original jurisdiction, §1836(c); the 2016 law struck "exclusive," so state courts likely share it `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Trade secret, §1839(3): any business, technical or other information, including "programs, or codes," tangible or intangible, where (A) the owner took reasonable secrecy measures and (B) it has independent economic value from not being generally known or readily ascertainable by proper means. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1839, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1839.htm]
- Misappropriation, §1839(5): (A) acquisition knowing or with reason to know it came by improper means, or (B) disclosure or use without consent by one who used improper means, knew of a duty to keep it secret or limit use (the usual employee theory), or learned it by accident before a material change of position. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1839, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1839.htm]
- Improper means, §1839(6): theft, bribery, misrepresentation, breach or inducing breach of a secrecy duty, espionage; excludes reverse engineering, independent derivation and other lawful means. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1839, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1839.htm]

### DTSA remedies and procedure
- Injunction against actual or threatened misappropriation, but it may not "prevent a person from entering into an employment relationship." [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(3)(A)(i), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Employment conditions must rest on evidence of threatened misappropriation, "not merely on the information the person knows"; no conflict with state laws barring restraints on a lawful profession. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(3)(A)(i), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Also: affirmative acts to protect the secret; royalty injunction only in exceptional circumstances. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(3)(A), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Damages: actual loss plus unjust enrichment not counted in loss, or a reasonable royalty; exemplary damages up to 2x for willful and malicious misappropriation. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(3)(B)–(C), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Fees to the prevailing party for a bad-faith claim, a bad-faith motion to terminate an injunction (made or opposed), or willful and malicious misappropriation. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(3)(D), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Ex parte civil seizure only in extraordinary circumstances: eight findings, including that a Rule 65 order would be evaded, immediate irreparable injury, likely success, actual possession, risk of destruction on notice, and no publicity by applicant. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(2), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Seizure order needs a hearing within 7 days and security; a federal officer executes it; wrongful-seizure claim available. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(2), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Limitations: 3 years from actual or constructive discovery; continuing misappropriation is a single claim. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(d), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]
- Applies where any act of misappropriation occurs on or after 2016-05-11 (Pub. L. 114-153 §2(e)). [cite: note to 18 U.S.C. §1833, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- No preemption except §1833(b): the DTSA stacks with state trade-secret claims; state displacement rules still govern the state counts `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1838, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1838.htm]
- Reaches conduct abroad if the offender is a US citizen, permanent resident or US organization, or an act in furtherance occurred in the US. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1837, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1837.htm]
- Currency: no amendment after 2016 in the govinfo 2024 edition or LII credits. `[UNVERIFIED: OLRC current text unreachable; 2025–26 public laws not checked there]` [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836 (2024 ed.), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm]

### DTSA whistleblower immunity and the employer notice, 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)
- No liability under "any Federal or State trade secret law" for disclosure (A) in confidence to a government official or an attorney solely to report or investigate a suspected violation of law, or (B) in a sealed court filing. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(1), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Retaliation suits: the individual may give the secret to their own attorney and use it in court if filed under seal and disclosed only by court order. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(2), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Notice duty: any employee agreement governing trade secrets or confidential information must give notice of the immunity; a cross-reference to a policy document given to the employee complies. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3)(A)–(B), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Sanction: no exemplary damages or attorney fees against an employee not given notice; applies to agreements entered into or updated after 2016-05-11. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3)(C)–(D), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- "Employee" includes any individual working as a contractor or consultant. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(4), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Immunity does not cover otherwise unlawful acts, such as unlawful access by unauthorized means. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(5), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Effect `[INFERENCE]`: missing notice does not defeat liability or an injunction; it removes only DTSA 2x and fees, only against the un-noticed worker; a re-signed or amended old form needs the notice. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- SEC Rule 21F-17(a): no person may act to impede communication with SEC staff about a possible securities violation, including by enforcing a confidentiality agreement; attorney-client carve-out. Reaches "any person," so any employer's NDA is exposed on securities-related information `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: 17 C.F.R. §240.21F-17, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-01/title-17.xml?part=240&section=240.21F-17]

### Economic Espionage Act (criminal), 18 U.S.C. §§1831–1832
- §1832: intent to convert a commerce-related trade secret to someone else's benefit, intending or knowing it will injure the owner, by theft, unauthorized copying, downloading, uploading or transmitting, or knowing receipt; attempt and conspiracy covered. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1832, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1832.htm]
- §1832 penalties: individuals up to 10 years; organizations the greater of $5M or 3x the secret's value, including avoided R&D. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1832, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1832.htm]
- §1831 (benefit to a foreign government or agent): individuals up to $5M and 15 years; organizations the greater of $10M or 3x value. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1831, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1831.htm]
- 2012 amendment (Pub. L. 112-236) widened the commerce element to products or services used or intended for use in commerce; it followed *Aleynikov*, which reversed an EEA conviction under the old wording (causal link `[INFERENCE]`). [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1832 notes, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1832.htm; *United States v. Aleynikov*, 676 F.3d 71 (2d Cir. 2012), https://static.case.law/f3d/676/cases/0071-01.json]
- National Stolen Property Act does not reach interstate transmission of purely intangible property; copied source code is not "goods." [cite: *Aleynikov*, 676 F.3d 71, https://static.case.law/f3d/676/cases/0071-01.json]
- §2314 covers goods, wares, merchandise, securities or money worth $5,000+; physical devices remain "goods" `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §2314, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap113-sec2314.htm]

### Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, 18 U.S.C. §1030
- Insider prongs: (a)(2)(C) access without authorization or exceeding authorized access to obtain information from a protected computer; (a)(4) access with intent to defraud; (a)(5)(A) transmitting code that intentionally causes damage; (a)(5)(B)–(C) unauthorized access that recklessly causes damage, or causes damage and loss. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1030, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap47-sec1030.htm]
- Definitions: "exceeds authorized access" (e)(6); "damage" = impairment to integrity or availability of data or systems (e)(8); "loss" = reasonable response, assessment and restoration costs plus interruption losses (e)(11); "protected computer" includes any used in or affecting interstate commerce (e)(2)(B). [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1030(e), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap47-sec1030.htm]
- Civil action, §1030(g): needs a (c)(4)(A)(i) factor, usually loss of at least $5,000 in one year (economic damages only under that factor); 2 years from the act or discovery of damage. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1030(g), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap47-sec1030.htm]
- *Van Buren*: exceeding authorized access means entering off-limits areas such as "files, folders, or databases" (slip op. pg. 20); a "gates-up-or-down inquiry" (pg. 13). [cite: *Van Buren v. United States*, 593 U.S. 374 (2021), https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf]
- *Van Buren* left open whether gates are only code-based or also contract/policy limits (pg. 13 n.8); rejected making every computer-use policy violation a crime (pg. 17). [cite: *Van Buren*, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf]
- *Van Buren*: damage and loss focus on technological harms like file corruption and are "ill fitted" to misuse of permitted information (pg. 15). [cite: *Van Buren*, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf]
- 4th Cir. (binding in SC): narrow reading of both access terms; rejects cessation-of-agency theory; employees who downloaded files to personal computers using access they had did not violate the CFAA; remedies lie in state law. [cite: *WEC Carolina Energy Solutions LLC v. Miller*, 687 F.3d 199 (4th Cir. 2012), https://static.case.law/f3d/687/cases/0199-01.json]
- 7th Cir. (binding in IN): (1) installing a secure-erase program to wipe an employer laptop was a "transmission" under (a)(5)(A); (2) breach of loyalty ended the employee's agency and authorization. [cite: *Int'l Airport Ctrs., L.L.C. v. Citrin*, 440 F.3d 418 (7th Cir. 2006), https://static.case.law/f3d/440/cases/0418-01.json]
- *Citrin* holding (2) looks inconsistent with *Van Buren*; holding (1), deliberate data destruction, still fits (a)(5)(A) `[INFERENCE]`. `[UNVERIFIED: no post-Van Buren 7th Cir. ruling found]` [cite: *Citrin*, https://static.case.law/f3d/440/cases/0418-01.json; *Van Buren*, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *NRA Group, LLC v. Durenleau*, No. 24-1123 (3d Cir.; filed 2025-08-26, amended opinion 2025-10-07, holdings unchanged): absent code-based hacking, current employees' breach of computer-use policies is not a CFAA claim. [cite: *NRA Group* slip op. pg. 4, 23–24, https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/241123p.pdf; rehearing order, https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/241123po.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *NRA Group* also held these passwords were not trade secrets (DTSA or Pa. law): no independent economic value of their own; value lay in what they protected (slip op. pg. 26–29). Employers should use contract, business-tort and fraud claims (pg. 24). [cite: *NRA Group*, https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/241123p.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** Certiorari denied 2026-03-30, No. 25-1033; the decision is final. Persuasive only in the 4th and 7th Circuits `[INFERENCE]`. Reporter cites `[UNVERIFIED: party filing and Leagle only]`. [cite: SCOTUS docket 25-1033, https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-1033.html]
- *NRA Group* facts: a coworker logged in for a sick colleague with permission and emailed a password spreadsheet; suit came "just weeks" after the employees resigned or were fired, following harassment complaints. [cite: *NRA Group*, https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/241123p.pdf]
- DOJ policy (May 2022): no "exceeds authorized access" charges where authorization rests on contract or policy terms (unless files are wholly off-limits), and none on automatic-withdrawal theories. [cite: Justice Manual 9-48.000, https://www.justice.gov/jm/jm-9-48000-computer-fraud]

## Indiana

### Indiana Uniform Trade Secrets Act, IC 24-2-3
- Trade secret: information, including a formula, pattern, compilation, program, device, method, technique or process, that (1) derives independent economic value from not being generally known or readily ascertainable by proper means, and (2) is the subject of reasonable secrecy efforts. [cite: IC 24-2-3-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Improper means and misappropriation track the uniform act; the improper-means definition has no express reverse-engineering exclusion. [cite: IC 24-2-3-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Injunction: actual or threatened misappropriation may be enjoined, extendable a reasonable period to remove commercial advantage; royalty injunction in exceptional circumstances; affirmative acts may be compelled. [cite: IC 24-2-3-3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Damages: actual loss plus unjust enrichment not counted in loss; a royalty only when neither is provable; exemplary damages up to twice the loss/enrichment award for willful and malicious misappropriation. [cite: IC 24-2-3-4 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Fees to the prevailing party: bad-faith claim, bad-faith motion to terminate an injunction (made or resisted), or willful and malicious misappropriation. [cite: IC 24-2-3-5 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Court must preserve secrecy: protective orders, in-camera hearings, sealing. [cite: IC 24-2-3-6 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Limitations: 3 years from actual or constructive discovery; continuing misappropriation is a single claim. [cite: IC 24-2-3-7 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Displacement: displaces conflicting Indiana law "pertaining to the misappropriation of trade secrets," except contract and criminal law. [cite: IC 24-2-3-1(c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Currency: last amendment to the chapter is P.L.137-2021 (§8 only); no 2025–26 change. [cite: IC 24-2-3-8 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]

### Indiana trade-secret cases
- Indiana adopted the UTSA substantially as promulgated, so other UTSA states' cases are relevant authority. [cite: *Amoco Prod. Co. v. Laird*, 622 N.E.2d 912 (Ind. 1993), https://static.case.law/ne2d/622/cases/0912-01.json]
- "Not readily ascertainable" where duplication takes a substantial investment of time, expense or effort; rejects an economic-infeasibility test; a compilation of partly public parts can qualify; plaintiff must identify and prove the secrets. [cite: *Amoco*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/622/cases/0912-01.json]
- *Amoco* treats the Restatement §757 factors as guidance and relied on ease of duplication. [cite: *Amoco*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/622/cases/0912-01.json]
- Indiana's displacement clause is broader than the uniform act's; it displaces respondeat superior, so a new employer is not vicariously liable for a hire's misappropriation without scienter (knew or had reason to know). [cite: *Infinity Prods., Inc. v. Quandt*, 810 N.E.2d 1028 (Ind. 2004), https://static.case.law/ne2d/810/cases/1028-01.json]
- *Quandt* counted locked offices, locked cabinets and computer passwords as secrecy measures; left open whether civil treble damages based on criminal acts fit the criminal-law exception. [cite: *Quandt*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/810/cases/1028-01.json]
- UTSA injunction may bar a departing executive from working for any competitor for one year, based on "pre-departure harvesting" of proprietary information. [cite: *Ackerman v. Kimball Int'l, Inc.*, 652 N.E.2d 507 (Ind. 1995), https://static.case.law/ne2d/652/cases/0507-01.json]
- Inevitable disclosure: a federal district court predicts Indiana limits it to a rare, narrow set of bad-faith taking cases; this is not an Indiana holding. [cite: *Dearborn v. Everett J. Prescott, Inc.*, 486 F. Supp. 2d 802 (S.D. Ind. 2007), https://static.case.law/f-supp-2d/486/cases/0802-01.json]
- Court of Appeals applied the same narrow approach, affirming denial of a preliminary injunction; inevitable-disclosure claim was "little more than speculation" without proof of download or transfer. `[UNVERIFIED: published/precedential status; text read via FindLaw copy]` [cite: *Med-1 Solutions, LLC v. Taylor*, No. 24A-PL-450 (Ind. Ct. App. Nov. 25, 2024), https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]

### Duty of loyalty (common law)
- Employee owes a duty of loyalty: no active, direct competition for customers or employees while employed; may prepare to compete but not use employer-peculiar confidential information before leaving. [cite: *Kopka, Landau & Pinkus v. Hansen*, 874 N.E.2d 1065 (Ind. Ct. App. 2007), https://static.case.law/ne2d/874/cases/1065-01.json]
- A loyalty claim resting only on misuse of secret information risks UTSA displacement `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: *HDNet*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/972/cases/0920-01.json]

### Criminal statutes (Title 35)
- "Property" throughout Title 35 includes intangibles, trade secrets and data; property is "of another person" even if the accused also has an interest. [cite: IC 35-31.5-2-253 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- "Exert control" includes obtain, take, conceal, possess and transfer; control is "unauthorized" if beyond the manner or extent consented to. [cite: IC 35-43-4-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- A former employee keeping a company laptop or copying files beyond permission fits these words `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: IC 35-43-4-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- Theft: knowing unauthorized control with intent to deprive of any part of value or use. Class A misdemeanor; Level 6 felony at $750 to under $50,000; Level 5 at $50,000+. [cite: IC 35-43-4-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- Criminal conversion: same unauthorized control without intent to deprive; Class A misdemeanor (vehicle enhancements aside). [cite: IC 35-43-4-3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- Offense against intellectual property: knowingly, without authorization, modifying or destroying data or programs, or disclosing or taking data that is a trade secret under IC 24-2-3-2 or confidential by law, on a computer or network. Level 6 felony; unchanged since 2014. [cite: IC 35-43-1-7 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- Offense against computer users: disrupting service, taking or damaging computer equipment, damaging a computer, or introducing a contaminant. Level 6 felony. [cite: IC 35-43-1-8(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- IC 35-43-1-8 grading: Level 5 at $750 to under $50,000 loss, a scheme to defraud, or impairing government or public services; Level 4 at $50,000+ or endangering life; Level 5 and 4 offenses are also AG-actionable deceptive acts. [cite: IC 35-43-1-8(b)–(c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- Computer trespass: knowingly accessing a computer system or network, or any part, without consent of the owner or the owner's licensee. Class A misdemeanor. "Access" includes retrieving data and using resources. [cite: IC 35-43-2-3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** P.L.111-2026 (SEA 119, signed 2026-03-05, effective 2026-07-01) added cellular phones and other wireless devices to "computer network" in computer trespass. [cite: IC 35-43-2-3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35; SEA 119 enrolled act, https://iga.in.gov/pdf-documents/124/2026/senate/bills/SB0119/SB0119.06.ENRH.pdf]
- Trespass uses a consent test, not *Van Buren*'s gates test `[INFERENCE]`. `[UNVERIFIED: no Indiana appellate reading after Van Buren found]` [cite: IC 35-43-2-3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- **CHANGED 2025–26 (not insider-relevant):** P.L.80-2026 added domestic animals to Level 6 theft. [cite: IC 35-43-4-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]

### Civil treble-damage statutes
- Crime Victims Relief Act: pecuniary loss from a violation of IC 35-43 (theft, conversion, IP offense, computer-user offense, trespass) allows up to 3x actual damages, costs, a reasonable attorney's fee, travel and time expenses, and other reasonable collection costs. [cite: IC 34-24-3-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34]
- Cannot recover both punitive damages and CVRA amounts. [cite: IC 34-24-3-3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34]
- **CHANGED 2025–26 (not insider-relevant):** P.L.142-2026 added IC 25-37.5 (valuable metals) to the CVRA predicate list. `[UNVERIFIED: effective date]` [cite: IC 34-24-3-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34]
- Corrupt business influence (RICO): conducting an enterprise's activities through a pattern of racketeering activity is a Level 5 felony. [cite: IC 35-45-6-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- Pattern: at least two interrelated incidents, the last within 5 years of a prior one; racketeering includes theft (IC 35-43-4-2) but not IC 35-43-1-7. [cite: IC 35-45-6-1(d)–(e) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]
- Civil RICO: on a preponderance, the court "shall order" 3x actual damages, costs, a reasonable fee and allowable punitive damages; jury right. [cite: IC 34-24-2-6(b)–(c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34]
- Civil RICO TRO or preliminary injunction needs a showing of immediate danger of significant loss or damage. [cite: IC 34-24-2-6(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34]
- **CHANGED 2025–26 (not insider-relevant):** P.L.186-2025 updated a drug cross-reference and P.L.148-2026 / P.L.153-2026 added sale of alcohol without a permit to the predicate list. [cite: IC 35-45-6-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35]

### Displacement of other Indiana claims
- UTSA displaces common-law idea misappropriation even for non-secret information (two-tier view: protected secret or unprotected general knowledge). [cite: *HDNet, LLC v. N. Am. Boxing Council*, 972 N.E.2d 920 (Ind. Ct. App. 2012), https://static.case.law/ne2d/972/cases/0920-01.json]
- *HDNet* also displaced a CVRA conversion claim over ideas (the CVRA is not "derivative" of criminal law as RICO is); information protected by contract is not displaced. Transfer denied `[UNVERIFIED: secondary report only]`. [cite: *HDNet*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/972/cases/0920-01.json]
- UTSA does not preempt civil RICO; injunction on UTSA and RICO grounds largely affirmed (one overbroad provision reversed) where ex-employees brought a competitor a process "library." [cite: *AGS Capital Corp. v. Prod. Action Int'l, LLC*, 884 N.E.2d 294 (Ind. Ct. App. 2008), https://static.case.law/ne2d/884/cases/0294-01.json]
- What survives `[INFERENCE]`: contract claims (NDA, return-of-property, invention assignment), criminal prosecution, civil RICO, and wrongs distinct from information misuse (retained device, data destruction, soliciting customers while employed). [cite: IC 24-2-3-1(c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24; *HDNet*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/972/cases/0920-01.json; *AGS*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/884/cases/0294-01.json]
- A CVRA claim for a tangible device does not "pertain to" trade secrets, so displacement should not apply `[INFERENCE]`; untested. [cite: IC 24-2-3-1(c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]

## South Carolina

### South Carolina Trade Secrets Act, S.C. Code §§39-8-10 to -130
- Improper means includes breaching duties imposed by common law, statute, contract, license or protective order; misappropriation includes plain acquisition by improper means. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-20(1)–(2), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Trade secret: value from not being generally known or readily ascertainable by the public or others, plus reasonable secrecy efforts; a series of minor items is judged by its collective effect. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-20(5), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Statutory employee duty: an employee who knows or should know of a trade secret must not use or disclose it without permission, independent of any NDA or non-compete. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-30(B), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- An NDA or use limit is not void for lacking a time or geographic limit. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-30(D), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Damages: actual loss or unjust enrichment, or a royalty, reducible for good-faith change of position; exemplary up to 2x for "wilful, wanton, or reckless disregard" (a lower bar than DTSA/Indiana). [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-40, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Injunction for actual or threatened misappropriation; extension accounts for average business growth; royalty injunctions; affirmative acts. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-50, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Fees for bad-faith claims, bad-faith injunction practice, or wilful misappropriation (no "malicious" needed). [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-80, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Discovery shield: before ordering discovery of designated secrets the court must find substantial need (particularized pleading, direct relevance, prejudice, likely admissibility); real-time database access generally barred. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-60, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- §39-8-60(B) applies in any civil action, alongside Rule 26(c) balancing. [cite: *Laffitte v. Bridgestone Corp.*, 381 S.C. 460 (2009), https://static.case.law/sc/381/cases/0460-01.json]
- Limitations: 3 years from discovery; continuing misappropriation is a single claim. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-70, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- First issue in every trade secret case is whether a trade secret exists. [cite: *Lowndes Prods., Inc. v. Brower*, 259 S.C. 322 (1972), https://static.case.law/sc/259/cases/0322-01.json]
- Currency: histories show 1997 Act No. 38 only; online Code current through the 2025 session. `[UNVERIFIED: 2026 session not checked; online Code unofficial]` [cite: S.C. Code ch. 39-8, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]

### Displacement, §39-8-110 and §39-8-30(E)
- Displaces conflicting tort, restitutionary and other civil-remedy law for trade-secret misappropriation; keeps contract remedies (even if based on misappropriation) and the SC Tort Claims Act; claims not based on misappropriation are unaffected. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-110, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- The chapter governs any and all civil remedies based on misappropriation or protection of a trade secret. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-30(E), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- SC uses the uniform act's narrower wording, not Indiana's "pertaining to" clause. `[UNVERIFIED: whether SC follows HDNet on non-secret information; no SC appellate case found]` [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-110, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]

### Criminal: §39-8-90 and the Computer Crime Act, §16-16-10 et seq.
- Trade-secret theft (steal, copy, download, upload, transmit, receive, solicit, attempt, conspire) with intent to injure the owner and benefit another: up to $100,000 and/or 10 years; Class E felony. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-90, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php; §16-1-90(E), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c001.php]
- Definitions: "computer" includes digital cellular phones; "property" includes data in transit; "unauthorized use" includes authorized use in a manner not authorized (a purpose test); hacking includes exceeding any permission granted. [cite: S.C. Code §16-16-10, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c016.php]
- Offense: wilfully, knowingly, maliciously, and without authorization or for an unauthorized purpose, accessing a computer to defraud, obtain property or commit a crime, or altering or destroying a computer or data. [cite: S.C. Code §16-16-20, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c016.php]
- Grading: first degree over $10,000 (felony, $50k / 5 years; Class F); second degree over $1,000 or depriving, taking, concealing or retaining any computer or data (misdemeanor, $10k / 1 year first offense); third degree $1,000 or less, or hacking; each computer is a separate violation. [cite: S.C. Code §16-16-20, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c016.php; §16-1-90, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c001.php]
- Civil action only against a person convicted under the chapter: compensatory damages, restitution, fees (verification, repair, lost profits, restoration); other civil remedies preserved. [cite: S.C. Code §16-16-25, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c016.php]
- SC's "unauthorized purpose" and "retains possession" wording reaches employee misuse the CFAA no longer does, but the civil route needs a conviction first `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: S.C. Code §§16-16-10, -20, -25, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c016.php]

### Larceny, breach of trust, conversion, loyalty, SCUTPA
- Petit larceny covers goods and choses in action of $2,000 or less; grand larceny above is a felony, up to 5 years (to $10k) or 10 years ($10k+). [cite: S.C. Code §16-13-30, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c013.php]
- Breach of trust with fraudulent intent is larceny at the same thresholds; fits an employee entrusted with devices or funds `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: S.C. Code §16-13-230, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c013.php]
- Civil conversion: unauthorized assumption of ownership over another's goods or chattels; identifiable money counts. `[UNVERIFIED: whether SC recognizes conversion of electronic data]` [cite: *Moore v. Weinberg*, 383 S.C. 583 (2009), https://static.case.law/sc/383/cases/0583-01.json]
- Conversion claims based on trade-secret misappropriation are displaced. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-110, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Employee owes a duty of fidelity; disloyal side work for a competitor justified discharge and forfeited release pay. [cite: *Berry v. Goodyear Tire & Rubber Co.*, 270 S.C. 489 (1978), https://static.case.law/sc/270/cases/0489-01.json]
- SCUTPA: unfair or deceptive acts in trade or commerce; private action needs ascertainable loss; court shall award 3x for willful or knowing violations, plus fees. `[UNVERIFIED: whether SCUTPA reaches employer–employee secret disputes or needs public-interest impact]` [cite: S.C. Code §§39-5-20, -140, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c005.php]
- A SCUTPA claim resting on misappropriation is likely displaced `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-110, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]

## Facts that decide outcomes
- Employer side: secrets identified with particularity; a vague 43-page description failed to separate secrets from other information. [cite: *IDX Sys. Corp. v. Epic Sys. Corp.*, 285 F.3d 581 (7th Cir. 2002), https://static.case.law/f3d/285/cases/0581-01.json; *Amoco*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/622/cases/0912-01.json]
- Employer side: documented secrecy measures (locked rooms, passwords, NDAs, access tiers, need-to-know); Restatement factors are guidelines, not a rigid test. [cite: *Quandt*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/810/cases/1028-01.json; *Learning Curve Toys, Inc. v. PlayWood Toys, Inc.*, 342 F.3d 714 (7th Cir. 2003), https://static.case.law/f3d/342/cases/0714-01.json]
- Employer side: high cost to recreate shows the information is not readily ascertainable. [cite: *Amoco*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/622/cases/0912-01.json]
- Employer side: forensic proof of taking (downloads, USB, personal email or cloud, wiping); harvesting won an employment bar, while missing download proof sank *Med-1*. [cite: *Ackerman*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/652/cases/0507-01.json; *Med-1*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]
- Employer side: code-based access limits for any CFAA claim (role permissions, revoked credentials, someone else's login); use of revoked credentials after departure fits "without authorization" `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: *Van Buren*, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf; *WEC*, https://static.case.law/f3d/687/cases/0199-01.json]
- Employer side: CFAA $5,000 loss built from response, assessment and restoration costs; technological harm required. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1030(e)(11), (g), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap47-sec1030.htm; *Van Buren* pg. 15, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf]
- Employer side: signed agreements carrying the §1833(b) notice keep DTSA 2x and fees available. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Employer side: in Indiana, prove the new employer knew or had reason to know; a prompt notice letter can create that knowledge `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: *Quandt*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/810/cases/1028-01.json]
- Employer side: timing; 3 years for trade-secret claims, 2 years for CFAA; delay undercuts irreparable harm `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(d), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm; IC 24-2-3-7 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24; S.C. Code §39-8-70, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Employer side: in SC, the statutory duty binds employees even without an NDA. [cite: S.C. Code §39-8-30(B), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Employee side: general skill, knowledge and public information are not secrets; in Indiana non-secret information supports no tort claim (contract claims survive). [cite: *HDNet*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/972/cases/0920-01.json]
- Employee side: weak secrecy practices (no access controls, broad sharing, no NDAs) defeat the reasonable-efforts element. [cite: IC 24-2-3-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24; 18 U.S.C. §1839(3), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1839.htm]
- Employee side: authorized access usually defeats the CFAA. [cite: *WEC*, https://static.case.law/f3d/687/cases/0199-01.json; *NRA Group*, https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/241123p.pdf]
- Employee side: no use or disclosure, prompt return and a certification of deletion undercut threatened misappropriation and inevitable disclosure `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: *Dearborn*, https://static.case.law/f-supp-2d/486/cases/0802-01.json]
- Employee side: no §1833(b) notice means no DTSA exemplary damages or fees against that worker. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3)(C), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Employee side: confidential disclosure to a lawyer or official, or under seal, is immune; NDAs cannot block SEC reporting; hacking to get the material is not immune. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm; 17 C.F.R. §240.21F-17, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-01/title-17.xml?part=240&section=240.21F-17]
- Employee side: attack displaced counts (Indiana conversion, idea and unjust-enrichment counts; SC tort counts based on misappropriation). [cite: *HDNet*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/972/cases/0920-01.json; S.C. Code §39-8-110, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Employee side: the DTSA cannot bar employment; weaker in Indiana because of *Ackerman*. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(3)(A)(i), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm; *Ackerman*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/652/cases/0507-01.json]
- Employee side: preparing to compete is lawful; active competition or using confidential information before leaving is not. [cite: *Kopka*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/874/cases/1065-01.json]
- Employee side: a suit filed soon after protected complaints draws scrutiny; *NRA Group* opens by calling the law in the wrong hands "a hammer in search of a nail." [cite: *NRA Group*, https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/241123p.pdf]

## Common offer-letter / situation risks and typical recommendations
- Confidentiality, NDA or invention agreement with no §1833(b) notice: likely High (loss of DTSA 2x and fees against that worker) when entered or updated after 2016-05-11; typical fix: add the immunity notice or cross-reference a reporting policy given to the worker, including contractors. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(3)–(4), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Confidentiality clause with no government-reporting carve-out: likely Med when the information could concern a possible securities violation; typical fix: carve out reports to the SEC and other agencies. [cite: 17 C.F.R. §240.21F-17, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-01/title-17.xml?part=240&section=240.21F-17]
- Employer plans a CFAA claim over files the employee could already open: likely High (claim fails) when access was authorized and only a policy was broken; typical fix: plead DTSA/state trade-secret and contract claims; build code-based access gates going forward. [cite: *Van Buren*, https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf; *WEC*, https://static.case.law/f3d/687/cases/0199-01.json]
- Blanket "all information is confidential" with no inventory: likely Med when litigated; typical fix: maintain a list of specific secrets and the measures protecting each. [cite: *IDX*, https://static.case.law/f3d/285/cases/0581-01.json; *Amoco*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/622/cases/0912-01.json]
- Weak access controls (shared logins, open drives): likely High risk of losing trade-secret status when information was widely shared; typical fix: access tiers, passwords, locked storage, NDAs. [cite: IC 24-2-3-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24; *Quandt*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/810/cases/1028-01.json]
- Offboarding without a device-return and return-and-certify process: likely Med when devices or files leave with the worker; typical fix: contract clause requiring return and a deletion certificate (contract claims survive displacement). [cite: IC 24-2-3-1(c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24; S.C. Code §39-8-110(B), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
- Departing worker keeps a company laptop or phone (Indiana): likely Med–High exposure when kept past a demand; typical fix: written demand, then theft/conversion and CVRA claim for the device. [cite: IC 35-43-4-2, -3 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35; IC 34-24-3-1 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34]
- Worker copies secret files to personal cloud or USB before leaving (Indiana): likely High when forensic proof exists (Level 6 felony exposure, possible employment bar); typical fix for the worker: stop, return, certify deletion, don't use. [cite: IC 35-43-1-7 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/35; *Ackerman*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/652/cases/0507-01.json]
- Same conduct in SC: likely High when the worker retains data or uses access for an unauthorized purpose; typical fix: as above; note the statutory duty applies without an NDA. [cite: S.C. Code §§16-16-20, 39-8-30(B), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t16c016.php]
- Hiring a competitor's employee: likely Med for the new employer when it knows or has reason to know of taken secrets; typical fix: onboarding certification that no prior-employer information is brought, and refuse any offered. [cite: *Quandt*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/810/cases/1028-01.json; 18 U.S.C. §1839(5)(A), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1839.htm]
- Employer seeks to stop a departing worker from taking a job under the DTSA alone: likely High (unavailable) on any facts; typical fix: pair with an Indiana UTSA claim on harvesting facts. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1836(b)(3)(A)(i), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1836.htm; *Ackerman*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/652/cases/0507-01.json]
- Inevitable-disclosure theory without proof of taking (Indiana): likely High (fails) when there is no download or transfer evidence; typical fix: gather forensics first. [cite: *Dearborn*, https://static.case.law/f-supp-2d/486/cases/0802-01.json; *Med-1*, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/in-court-of-appeals/116719877.html]
- Employee holding documents for a whistleblower report: likely Low when shared only in confidence with a lawyer or official, or under seal; High if obtained by unauthorized access. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1833(b)(1), (b)(5), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap90-sec1833.htm]
- Suing soon after the worker's protected complaints: likely Med scrutiny risk when timing is close `[INFERENCE]`; typical fix: document the forensic basis before filing. [cite: *NRA Group*, https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/241123p.pdf]
- Delay after discovering a taking: likely Med when months pass before suit; typical fix: calendar 3-year and 2-year deadlines and move promptly for any injunction `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: 18 U.S.C. §1030(g), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap47-sec1030.htm; IC 24-2-3-7 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]

## Creative angles (non-obvious, lawful)
- Indiana civil RICO for mandatory 3x: two or more related thefts of data, secrets or devices within 5 years can form a pattern, and civil RICO survives UTSA displacement. - Pair DTSA with Indiana UTSA to get an employment bar the DTSA forbids. Risk: medium; needs bad-faith taking. Enforceability: short, limited bar on harvesting facts `[INFERENCE]`. [cite: *Ackerman*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/652/cases/0507-01.json; *Dearborn*, https://static.case.law/f-supp-2d/486/cases/0802-01.json]
- Indiana CVRA treble damages for retained hardware, pleaded as a tangible-property wrong separate from the data claim. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/34; *HDNet*, https://static.case.law/ne2d/972/cases/0920-01.json]

Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24; S.C. Code §39-8-30(D), §39-8-110(B), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c008.php]
