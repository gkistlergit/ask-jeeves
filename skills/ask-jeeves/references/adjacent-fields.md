# Adjacent fields of law: reference

> For issue-spotting only. Not legal advice. Every claim cites its source; [UNVERIFIED] marks claims that could not be confirmed.

Scope (ticket 16): built-in coverage for five fields that change what a confidentiality, separation or assignment clause may say, or add money exposure in the same exit: **whistleblower**, **wage payment and wage-hour**, **discrimination and retaliation**, **IP ownership**, **data-breach notification (narrow)**. NLRA, unemployment, tax and immigration get a one-line flag only (see "Deferred fields"). Workplace AI and the Indiana CDPA are excluded as fields (see "Excluded"). Antitrust no-poach and the FTC non-compete rule belong to the restrictive-covenant reference, not here.

## Federal

### Whistleblower: DTSA immunity and notice
- An individual is immune for disclosing a trade secret in confidence to a government official or an attorney solely to report a suspected violation, or in a sealed court filing. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(1), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- Any contract governing trade secrets or confidential information must give notice of that immunity. Without it, the employer cannot recover exemplary damages or attorney fees against that employee. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3)(A), (C), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- The notice rule applies to contracts entered into or updated after May 11, 2016. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3)(D), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- "Employee" for the notice rule includes contractors and consultants. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(4), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- The immunity does not authorize "unlawful access of material by unauthorized means." It covers disclosure, not hacking or unauthorized taking. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(5), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]

### Whistleblower: SEC Rule 21F-17
- No person may impede anyone from contacting SEC staff about a possible securities violation, including by enforcing or threatening to enforce a confidentiality agreement. It reaches any employer's agreements, not only public companies'. [cite: 17 C.F.R. 240.21F-17(a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.21F-17]
- On Jan 16, 2025 the SEC found Two Sigma violated the rule with separation agreements requiring departing employees to certify they had filed no agency complaints ($90 million total penalties in that matter). [cite: SEC Press Release 2025-15, https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15]
- [UNVERIFIED: whether the SEC brought any 21F-17 case after Jan 16, 2025; not checked.]

### Whistleblower: SOX (public companies)
- 18 U.S.C. 1514A protects employees of companies with registered securities who report fraud. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1514A, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1514A]
- A complaint goes to the Secretary of Labor within 180 days. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1514A(b)(2)(D), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1514A]
- The rights "may not be waived by any agreement," and pre-dispute arbitration of these claims is unenforceable. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1514A(e), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1514A]

### Whistleblower: DOJ awards pilot
- DOJ's Criminal Division pays awards for original information that leads to forfeiture. A company that self-reports within 120 days of an internal report may be eligible for a declination. [cite: DOJ Corporate Whistleblower Awards Pilot, https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-division-corporate-whistleblower-awards-pilot-program]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** A May 12, 2025 Criminal Division memo added subject areas including trade, tariff and customs fraud, immigration offenses, sanctions and cartel offenses; the pilot page lists them, with guidance dated July 30, 2026 (page updated Oct 6, 2026). [cite: DOJ memo of May 12, 2025, https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1400141/dl; pilot page, https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-division-corporate-whistleblower-awards-pilot-program]

### Wage-hour: FLSA
- Wages must be paid "free and clear." A required kickback, such as tools of the trade, that cuts into minimum or overtime wages violates the rule. This is the floor for any stay-or-pay or equipment deduction. [cite: 29 C.F.R. 531.35, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.35]
- Retaliation for filing an FLSA complaint is unlawful. [cite: 29 U.S.C. 215(a)(3), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/215]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** DOL's Wage and Hour Division stopped applying the 2024 independent-contractor rule in its own enforcement and uses Fact Sheet #13 (2008) and Opinion Letter FLSA2019-6 instead; the 2024 rule stays in effect for private litigation. [cite: DOL FAB 2025-1 (May 1, 2025), https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/fab/fab2025-1.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** DOL proposed rescinding the 2024 rule (NPRM announced Feb 26, 2026, 91 FR 9932). No final rule appears on DOL's rulemaking page as of 2026-10-20. Matters when a "contractor" signed a non-compete. [cite: DOL WHD rulemaking page, https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa/misclassification/rulemaking]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** 26 U.S.C. 225 creates a deduction for qualified overtime pay (cap $12,500, or $25,000 joint) for tax years 2025–2028, enacted July 4, 2025. It raises the stakes of an exempt-classification challenge. [cite: 26 U.S.C. 225, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/225]

### Discrimination and retaliation
- Title VII bars retaliation against anyone who opposed a practice it prohibits or took part in a proceeding under it. [cite: 42 U.S.C. 2000e-3(a), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/2000e-3]
- EEOC guidance (Aug 25, 2016) lists "filing a civil action" as a materially adverse action, so a covenant suit filed after an EEOC charge can be attacked as retaliation. The guidance shows no rescission note but has no force of law. [cite: EEOC Retaliation Guidance §II.B.2, https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-retaliation-and-related-issues]
- Retaliation was alleged in 54,350 of 88,201 EEOC charges in FY 2025 (61.6%), the most of any basis. [cite: EEOC Table E1a, https://www.eeoc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/Table_E1a._Charge_Receipts_by_Basis_or_Statute_%28All_Statutes%29_FY_1997_-_FY_2025_0.xlsx]
- **OWBPA:** a release of age claims is valid only if it is written, names the ADEA, gives extra consideration, and advises in writing to see a lawyer. The employee gets 21 days to consider (45 for a group program) and 7 days to revoke; group programs need age disclosures. [cite: 29 U.S.C. 626(f)(1), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/626]
- **Speak Out Act:** a pre-dispute non-disclosure or non-disparagement clause is not judicially enforceable in a sexual assault or sexual harassment dispute (Pub. L. 117-224, Dec 7, 2022). It cuts into broad confidentiality clauses in offer letters. [cite: 42 U.S.C. 19403, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/19403]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** The EEOC regained a quorum when a third commissioner was sworn in on Oct 27, 2025. [cite: EEOC release, https://www.eeoc.gov/newsroom/brittany-bull-panuccio-begins-tenure-eeoc-commissioner]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** The EEOC rescinded its 2024 harassment guidance by a 2–1 vote on Jan 22, 2026 (release dated Jan 23). The Chair said this "does not give employers license" to harass. [cite: EEOC release, https://www.eeoc.gov/newsroom/eeoc-commission-votes-rescind-2024-harassment-guidance]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *Ames v. Ohio Dept. of Youth Services*, No. 23-1039 (June 5, 2025), rejected the "background circumstances" rule for majority-group plaintiffs. [cite: Ames slip op., https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/23-1039_c0n2.pdf]

### IP ownership
- An inventor owns the invention by default. *Stanford v. Roche*, 563 U.S. 776 (June 6, 2011), held the Bayh-Dole Act does not automatically vest title in federal contractors. [cite: Stanford v. Roche, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/09-1159]
- In *Stanford*, the inventor "agree[d] to assign" to Stanford but "do[es] hereby assign" to Cetus, and Cetus's assignment controlled the outcome. [cite: Stanford v. Roche, syllabus, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/09-1159] [UNVERIFIED: the general rule that "hereby assign" is a present transfer and "agree to assign" a promise comes from Federal Circuit cases not opened; confirm in the IP or contracts reference.]
- Patent assignments must be in writing. An unrecorded assignment can lose to a later purchaser unless recorded within three months. [cite: 35 U.S.C. 261, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/261]
- In a work made for hire, the employer "is considered the author." [cite: 17 U.S.C. 201(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/201]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, No. 23-5233 (D.C. Cir. Mar 18, 2025), held the Copyright Act requires a human author (pg. 3). Material generated entirely by AI may carry no copyright for an assignment clause to transfer. [cite: Thaler, https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2025/03/23-5233.pdf]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** The Supreme Court denied certiorari in *Thaler* on Mar 2, 2026 (No. 25-449). [cite: S. Ct. docket 25-449, https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-449.html]

## Indiana

### Whistleblower
- An employee of a private employer under public contract may report in writing a violation of federal, state or local law, or misuse of public resources, concerning that contract. The report goes first to the employer unless the employer is the wrongdoer. [cite: IC 22-5-3-3(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- If no good-faith effort to correct follows within a reasonable time, the employee may report to anyone. [cite: IC 22-5-3-3(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- For that report the employee may not be dismissed, denied raises or benefits, transferred, denied a promotion or demoted. Knowingly false reports can be disciplined, with appeal as a civil action. [cite: IC 22-5-3-3(b), (c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- A violation is a Class A infraction. [cite: IC 22-5-3-3(d) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- [UNVERIFIED: no general private-sector whistleblower statute was found in Indiana (negative search result).]

### Wage payment: penalties and timing
- An employer that fails to pay wages owes the unpaid wages, and the court "shall order" a reasonable attorney fee and costs. [cite: IC 22-2-5-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- If the employer was not acting in good faith, the court shall order liquidated damages of "two (2) times the amount of wages due." [cite: IC 22-2-5-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- When an employer separates an employee, unpaid wages are due at the regular payday for the pay period in which separation occurred (railroads excepted). [cite: IC 22-2-9-2(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- "Wages" for the Wage Claims chapter includes time, task, piece and commission pay. [cite: IC 22-2-9-1(b) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]

### Wage payment: deductions
- A wage assignment (deduction) is valid only if it is in writing, signed by the employee personally, revocable at any time on written notice, agreed to in writing by the employer, delivered to the employer within 10 days, and made for a listed purpose. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Listed purposes include uniforms and job-related clothing, and equipment or tools needed for the job, each at no more than the direct cost paid to an outside vendor. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b)(14), (15) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Uniform and tool assignments together may not exceed the lesser of $2,500 a year or 5% of weekly disposable earnings (the IN DOL states $48.08 a week). [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(d) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22; IN DOL, https://www.in.gov/dol/wage-and-hour/wage-and-hour-home/]
- Protective equipment, including PPE, cannot be charged through those assignments, except as the cited OSHA parts provide. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(e) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Education or skills-training reimbursement is a listed purpose, unless the training was funded in whole or part by a federal, state or local economic development incentive. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b)(16) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Payroll and vacation advances, and employer loans evidenced by a signed instrument, are listed purposes. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b)(7), (17) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Damage to or loss of company property is **not** a listed purpose. A stay-or-pay deduction can be lawful with a proper assignment; a deduction for stolen or unreturned property likely is not. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** The 2026 code carries two versions of IC 22-2-6-2, amended by P.L.104-2026 and P.L.115-2026. One adds a political-subdivision retirement purpose; the other updates consumer-credit cross-references. The purposes above are the same in both. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]

Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22] (Seen in the official text; not analyzed in the research. See open questions.)

### Data-breach notification
- "Breach of the security of data" is unauthorized acquisition of computerized data that compromises the security, confidentiality or integrity of personal information; it includes data moved to paper or other media. [cite: IC 24-4.9-2-2(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Not a breach: an employee's or agent's good-faith acquisition for the owner's lawful purposes, if the data is not used or further disclosed without authorization; also an encrypted device whose key is not compromised. [cite: IC 24-4.9-2-2(b) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- The database owner must notify affected Indiana residents when it knows or should know the breach has resulted or could result in identity deception, identity theft or fraud. [cite: IC 24-4.9-3-1(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Notice to more than 1,000 consumers also goes to each consumer reporting agency; any resident notice also goes to the attorney general. [cite: IC 24-4.9-3-1(b), (c) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Notice is due without unreasonable delay and "not more than forty-five (45) days" after discovery. Delay is reasonable to restore the system, find the scope, or on an AG or law-enforcement request. [cite: IC 24-4.9-3-3(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- A person that maintains data but does not own it must notify the database owner. [cite: IC 24-4.9-3-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]

### Indiana CDPA (narrow: receiving business only)
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** The Indiana Consumer Data Protection Act took effect Jan 1, 2026. [cite: IN AG CDPA guide, https://www.in.gov/attorneygeneral/files/Indiana-Consumer-Data-Protection_Consumer-Bill-of-Rights_CDPCBOR.pdf]
- It applies to a business that, in a calendar year, controls or processes personal data of at least 100,000 Indiana consumers, or 25,000 with more than 50% of gross revenue from selling personal data. [cite: IC 24-15-1-1(a) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- A "consumer" acts only for personal, family or household purposes and does not include someone "acting in a commercial or employment context." [cite: IC 24-15-2-8 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]
- Data processed about an applicant, employee, agent or contractor within that role is exempt, so the CDPA matters only when a departing employee brings Indiana consumers' data to a new business that meets the thresholds. [cite: IC 24-15-1-2(13) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]

## South Carolina

### Whistleblower
- The Whistleblower Act covers public bodies and publicly funded organizations, not "nonpublic, private corporations." [cite: S.C. Code 8-27-50, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t08c027.php]
- Actual damages are capped at $15,000. Reinstatement and lost wages carry no dollar cap. Fees are capped at $10,000 (trial) and $5,000 (appeal). [cite: S.C. Code 8-27-30(A), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t08c027.php]

### Wage payment
- The employer "shall not withhold or divert any portion of an employee's wages" without legal authority or written notice. [cite: S.C. Code 41-10-40(C), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- On separation, wages are due within 48 hours or by the next regular payday, and no later than 30 days. [cite: S.C. Code 41-10-50, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- Damages are "three times the full amount of the unpaid wages," plus costs and fees, with a 3-year limit. [cite: S.C. Code 41-10-80(C), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- "Wages" includes vacation and holiday pay owed under policy or contract. [cite: S.C. Code 41-10-10(2), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]

### Discrimination and retaliation
- The Human Affairs Law covers employers of 15 or more. [cite: S.C. Code 1-13-30(e), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t01c013.php]
- It bars retaliation. [cite: S.C. Code 1-13-80(F), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t01c013.php]
- A charge must be filed within 180 days. [cite: S.C. Code 1-13-90(a), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t01c013.php]

### Data-breach notification
- Unauthorized access to and acquisition of unencrypted data is a breach. An employee's good-faith acquisition is not a breach if the data is not misused or further disclosed. [cite: S.C. Code 39-1-90(D)(1), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c001.php]

## Deferred fields (flag only)

One line each in the memo's Adjacent issues. No analysis.

- **NLRA:** for a non-supervisory employee, severance terms requiring broad waiver of NLRA rights (confidentiality, non-disparagement) are unlawful under *McLaren Macomb*, still current law but under challenge in GC 26-04 (Aug 26, 2026). [cite: NLRB GC 26-04, https://apps.nlrb.gov/link/document.aspx/09031d4584351740]
- **Unemployment:** a discharge for rule violations or "any breach of duty" can bar benefits, and the benefits hearing may make an early record of the same facts. [cite: IC 22-4-15-1(d) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- **Tax on severance:** severance is FICA wages; route 409A timing and the 162(q) NDA rule to a tax professional. [cite: United States v. Quality Stores (2014), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/12-1408]
- **Immigration:** an H-1B worker may not be made to pay a penalty for leaving early; only bona fide liquidated damages are allowed. [cite: 20 C.F.R. 655.731(c)(10)(i), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/655.731]

## Excluded (and why)

- **Workplace AI is not its own field.** No IN or SC AI-employment statute was found [UNVERIFIED: negative search result], and the federal AI order of Jan 23, 2025 (EO 14179) revoked EO 14110. Its two real overlaps live elsewhere: AI-tool use and trade-secret reasonable measures (insider-theft reference), and human authorship (IP ownership above). [cite: EO 14179, https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2025-02172.json]

[cite: IC 24-15-1-2(13) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24]

## Facts that decide outcomes

- Employer side:
  - Was each confidentiality, invention or separation agreement entered into or updated after May 11, 2016, and does it carry the §1833(b) notice?
  - Does any clause or exit certification bar or discourage reports to the SEC or other agencies?
  - Is the company public (SOX), under an Indiana public contract (IC 22-5-3-3), or publicly funded in SC (8-27)?
  - Was final pay, a commission, bonus or vacation pay held back? On what date, and under what signed assignment?
  - What is the deduction for: training (a listed IN purpose) or property damage (not listed)? Was the training incentive-funded?
  - Headcount: six or more in Indiana (Civil Rights Law); 15 or more in SC (Human Affairs Law).
  - Does the release cover age claims, and is it part of a group exit program (45/7 days and disclosures)?
  - Does the dispute involve sexual harassment or assault allegations (Speak Out Act)?
  - Did the employee file a charge or complaint before the employer sued or threatened suit?
  - Does the assignment clause use present-assignment wording, and was any patent assignment recorded within three months?
  - Was the work human-authored, or generated wholly by AI?
  - Did the taken data include residents' personal information, and how many residents (more than 1,000)? When was it discovered (IN 45 days)?
- Employee side:
  - How were the documents obtained: through authorized access, or by unauthorized means (§1833(b)(5))?
  - To whom were they disclosed: a government official or attorney in confidence, a sealed filing, or a competitor?
  - Did the employer show good faith in withholding pay (IN 2x turns on it)?
  - Was the worker classified as a contractor, and does the work-for-hire clause fit that status?
  - Were prior inventions listed and carved out of the assignment?
  - Was a protected complaint (EEOC, SEC, FLSA, Indiana public-contract report) made, and when, relative to the adverse action?
  - Does the employee hold an H-1B visa, a non-supervisory role (NLRA), or a pending benefits claim (deferred flags)?

## Common offer-letter / situation risks and typical recommendations

- Confidentiality or invention agreement with no §1833(b) notice: likely **High** that the employer loses exemplary damages and fees when the agreement was entered or updated after May 11, 2016; typical fix: add the notice to every agreement covering confidential information, including contractor agreements. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3), (4), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- Clause or exit certification that bars agency contact or requires certifying no complaints: likely **High** under 21F-17 when it reaches SEC reports; typical fix: express carve-out for reports to the SEC and other agencies. [cite: 17 C.F.R. 240.21F-17, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.21F-17; SEC 2025-15, https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15]
- Arbitration or waiver clause applied to SOX claims at a public company: likely **High** that it is unenforceable; typical fix: carve SOX claims out. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1514A(e), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1514A]
- Withholding final pay to offset stolen or unreturned property (IN): likely **High** when no valid assignment exists, because property loss is not a listed purpose; exposure is wages plus fees, and 2x if not in good faith; typical fix: pay in full on the regular payday and pursue the property claim separately. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(b) and IC 22-2-5-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- Withholding final pay to offset property (SC): likely **High** when there is no legal authority or written notice; exposure is 3x plus fees; typical fix: pay within 48 hours or next payday and sue separately. [cite: S.C. Code 41-10-40(C), -50, -80(C), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- Stay-or-pay training repayment taken from final pay (IN): likely **Med** when a valid signed, revocable assignment exists for non-incentive-funded training; **High** without one; typical fix: a compliant assignment, and keep pay above the FLSA free-and-clear floor. ## Creative angles (non-obvious, lawful)

- **Cure a missing DTSA notice going forward.** The notice rule reaches contracts "updated" after May 11, 2016, so add the notice whenever an agreement is amended (a raise, promotion or new equity grant). Risk: it does not restore remedies for past agreements; state law may require consideration for the update. Enforceability: high for the notice itself. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3)(D), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- **Employer: pay first, claim later.** Pay final wages in full and on time, then pursue unreturned property or theft by separate claim. This trades a small offset for avoiding a 2x (IN) or 3x (SC) wage claim with fees. Risk: collecting later costs more. Enforceability: lawful by design. [cite: IC 22-2-5-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22; S.C. Code 41-10-80(C), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- **Indiana stay-or-pay done right.** Take a signed, revocable training-reimbursement assignment at enrollment, not at exit. Risk: the employee can revoke on written notice, and the assignment is barred for incentive-funded training. Enforceability: Med; check the FLSA floor and the H-1B penalty bar. [cite: IC 22-2-6-2(a), (b)(16) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22]
- **Employee: report through the immunity channel, not the side door.** A disclosure in confidence to a government official or an attorney, solely to report a suspected violation, is immune; giving the same file to a competitor or obtaining it by unauthorized means is not. Risk: "solely" and how the documents were obtained will be contested. Enforceability: statutory. [cite: 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(1), (5), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1833]
- **Employee: wage leverage in exit talks.** Where the employer offset final pay, the 2x (IN, bad faith) or 3x (SC) exposure plus fees is real leverage against a covenant demand. Risk: IN 2x needs a no-good-faith finding. Enforceability: statutory. [cite: IC 22-2-5-2 (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/22; S.C. Code 41-10-80(C), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c010.php]
- **Breach notice: test the good-faith exception before notifying.** Both states exclude an employee's good-faith acquisition when the data is not misused or further disclosed. Prompt return of the data with a written non-use statement builds that record. Risk: an employee taking data for a competitor likely was not acting for the owner's lawful purposes, so the exception may fail and late notice adds exposure. Enforceability: a judgment for counsel, made inside the 45-day window. [cite: IC 24-4.9-2-2(b)(1) (2026 Ind. Code, official IGA edition), https://iga.in.gov/laws/2026/ic/titles/24; S.C. Code 39-1-90(D)(1), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t39c001.php]
- **IP: log human authorship.** Require AI-use notes in work and invention disclosures so the human-authored parts that can carry copyright are identifiable. Risk: the log may show some work is not protectable. Enforceability: a policy choice, lawful. [cite: Thaler v. Perlmutter, https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2025/03/23-5233.pdf]
