# Adjacent fields of law: ranking

Ticket: `.scratch/ask-jeeves/issues/05-adjacent-fields-ranking.md`. Law current as of 2026-10-20. Scope: federal, Indiana (IN), South Carolina (SC). All sources accessed 2026-10-20. Not legal advice.

## Summary

Ten candidate fields (and one extra) were checked for how they meet the three priority fields: insider theft, restrictive covenants and employee contracts. The ones to build in are those that **change what a confidentiality, separation or assignment clause can lawfully say**, or that **create money exposure in the same exit event**. Fields that only sometimes apply, or that need a different professional, are flagged only.

## 1. Whistleblower protection — Include

**Intersection.** This is the closest adjacent field, because it limits what confidentiality clauses can do and covers employees who take documents to report wrongdoing.

- **DTSA immunity notice.** An individual is immune for disclosing a trade secret in confidence to a government official or an attorney solely to report a suspected violation, or in a sealed court filing [S19]. An employer must give notice of the immunity in any contract that governs trade secrets or confidential information. Without it, the employer cannot recover exemplary damages or attorney fees against that employee [S19, §1833(b)(3)(C)]. This applies to contracts entered into or updated after enactment (2016) [S19, §1833(b)(3)(D)]. "Employee" includes contractors and consultants [S19, §1833(b)(4)]. The immunity does not authorize "unlawful access of material by unauthorized means" [S19, §1833(b)(5)]. That limit matters for insider theft: the immunity covers disclosure, not hacking to get the documents. Ticket 01 covers the DTSA in full. Here it is the bridge into whistleblower law.
- **SEC Rule 21F-17.** No person may act to impede someone from contacting SEC staff about a possible securities violation, including by "enforcing, or threatening to enforce, a confidentiality agreement" [S20]. This reaches any employer's agreements, not only public companies'. On Jan 16, 2025 the SEC found Two Sigma violated the rule with separation agreements requiring departing employees to certify they had filed no agency complaints ($90 million total penalties in that matter) [S68]. UNVERIFIED: whether the SEC brought 21F-17 cases after that date under current leadership.
- **SOX (public companies).** 18 U.S.C. 1514A protects employees of companies with registered securities who report fraud [S21]. A complaint goes to the Secretary of Labor within 180 days [S21, §1514A(b)(2)(D)]. The rights "may not be waived by any agreement," and pre-dispute arbitration of these claims is unenforceable [S21, §1514A(e)].
- **DOJ awards program.** The Criminal Division's Corporate Whistleblower Awards Pilot pays for original information that leads to forfeiture [S22]. A company that self-reports within 120 days of an internal report may be eligible for a declination [S22]. **CHANGED 2025–26:** the page lists priority areas added by a May 12, 2025 memo [S67], and its guidance is dated July 30, 2026 [S22].
- **Indiana.** IC 22-5-3-3 protects employees of a private employer "under public contract" who report violations tied to that contract [S72][S23]. A violation is a Class A infraction [S72][S23]. (Official: the Indiana DOL page reproduces the section [S72].) No general private-sector whistleblower statute was found (UNVERIFIED negative).
- **South Carolina.** The Whistleblower Act covers public bodies and publicly funded organizations, not "nonpublic, private corporations" [S24, §8-27-50]. Actual damages are capped at $15,000; reinstatement and lost wages are listed without a dollar cap; fees are capped at $10,000 for trial and $5,000 for appeal [S24, §8-27-30(A)].

**How often.** The DTSA notice question comes up in every agreement that governs confidential information [S19]. Opinion counts were too small to use.

**Recommendation: Include.** Build in a short check: does the agreement have the §1833(b) notice, and does it carve out reports to the SEC and other agencies? Flag document-taking that may be protected whistleblowing, and note the §1833(b)(5) limit.

**Key authorities:** 18 U.S.C. 1833(b) [S19]; 17 C.F.R. 240.21F-17 [S20]; 18 U.S.C. 1514A [S21]; IC 22-5-3-3 [S23]; S.C. Code 8-27-10 to -50 [S24].

## 2. Wage payment and wage-hour — Include

**Intersection.** At separation, employers often hold back final pay, commissions or bonuses to offset stolen property, unreturned equipment or training-repayment ("stay-or-pay") clauses. State wage statutes punish this.

- **Indiana penalties.** An employer that fails to pay is liable for the unpaid wages, plus attorney fees. If it did not act in good faith, it owes liquidated damages of "two (2) times the amount of wages due" [S70][S71] (IC 22-2-5-2; official: quoted verbatim in an Ind. Ct. App. opinion [S70] and summarized by the Indiana DOL [S71]; FindLaw copy [S2]). For separated employees, unpaid wages are due at the regular payday for the pay period in which separation occurred [S4] (IC 22-2-9-2(a); FindLaw, secondary (official source not reachable)).
- **Indiana deductions.** A deduction needs a written, signed, revocable wage assignment for a listed purpose [S3] (IC 22-2-6-2). Listed purposes include tools and equipment, and uniforms, at direct cost. Together those two are capped at the lesser of $2,500 a year or 5% of weekly disposable earnings. The list also allows education or skills-training reimbursement, unless the training was funded by an economic development incentive program, and payroll or vacation advances [S3]. (Official in part: the Indiana DOL page confirms the uniform and equipment purposes, the $2,500-or-5% cap, the training exception and advances [S71]; the writing, signature and revocability conditions rest on FindLaw [S3], secondary (official source not reachable).) Damage to or loss of company property is not a listed purpose [S3] (FindLaw, secondary (official source not reachable)). So a stay-or-pay deduction may be lawful in Indiana with a proper assignment, but a deduction for stolen property likely is not.
- **South Carolina.** No deduction without legal authority or written notice; the employer "shall not withhold or divert any portion of an employee's wages" [S1] (S.C. Code 41-10-40(C)). Final wages are due within 48 hours or by the next payday, and no later than 30 days [S1] (§41-10-50). Damages are "three times the full amount of the unpaid wages," plus costs and fees, with a 3-year limit [S1] (§41-10-80(C)). Wages include vacation and holiday pay owed under policy or contract [S1] (§41-10-10).
- **Federal (FLSA).** Wages must be paid "free and clear." A required kickback of tools of the trade that cuts into minimum or overtime wages violates the rule [S5] (29 C.F.R. 531.35). Retaliation for filing a complaint is unlawful [S6] (29 U.S.C. 215(a)(3)).
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** DOL's Wage and Hour Division no longer applies the 2024 independent-contractor rule in its investigations. It uses Fact Sheet #13 (2008) and Opinion Letter FLSA2019-6 instead, but the 2024 rule "remains in effect" for private litigation [S7] (FAB 2025-1, May 1, 2025). DOL proposed rescinding the 2024 rule (NPRM announced Feb 26, 2026, 91 FR 9932); no final rule appears on DOL's rulemaking page as of 2026-10-20 [S65]. This matters when a "contractor" signed a non-compete. **CHANGED 2025–26:** 26 U.S.C. 225 creates a deduction for qualified FLSA overtime pay (cap $12,500, or $25,000 joint) for tax years 2025–2028 [S8]. That raises the stakes when an exempt classification is challenged.

**How often.** Across all years, 15 of 422 Indiana covenant or trade-secret opinions also mention wage-payment or FLSA terms (3.6%). This is the only opinion count that came back in usable size (see "How frequency was measured").

**Recommendation: Include.** Build in: final-pay timing and penalties (IN 2x, SC 3x), lawful deductions (IN wage-assignment list; SC notice rule), the FLSA free-and-clear floor for stay-or-pay, and contractor classification.

**Key authorities:** IC 22-2-5-2 [S2]; IC 22-2-6-2 [S3]; S.C. Code 41-10-40/-50/-80 [S1]; 29 C.F.R. 531.35 [S5]; FAB 2025-1 [S7].

## 3. Discrimination and retaliation — Include

**Intersection.** Separation agreements release these claims. Employees sued on a covenant often answer with retaliation claims. Confidentiality and non-disparagement clauses meet the Speak Out Act.

- **Retaliation.** Title VII bars retaliation against anyone who "has opposed any practice" it prohibits or who took part in a proceeding [S10] (42 U.S.C. 2000e-3(a)). The EEOC's retaliation guidance lists "filing a civil action" as a materially adverse action [S12, §II.B.2]. A covenant suit filed after an EEOC charge can be attacked as retaliation. The guidance (2016) shows no rescission note as of today, but it has no force of law [S12].
- **OWBPA (age claims in releases).** A release of age claims is valid only if it is written, names the ADEA, gives extra consideration, and advises seeing a lawyer in writing. The employee must get 21 days to consider (45 for a group program) and 7 days to revoke, and group programs need age disclosures [S9] (29 U.S.C. 626(f)(1)).
- **Speak Out Act.** A pre-dispute non-disclosure or non-disparagement clause is not judicially enforceable in a sexual assault or sexual harassment dispute [S11] (42 U.S.C. 19403). This cuts into broad confidentiality clauses in offer letters.
- **Indiana.** The Civil Rights Law covers employers of six or more people in Indiana [S73][S18] (IC 22-9-1-3(h); official: Indiana Civil Rights Commission [S73]).
- **South Carolina.** The Human Affairs Law covers employers of 15 or more [S17] (§1-13-30(e)), bars retaliation [S17] (§1-13-80(F)), and gives 180 days to file [S17] (§1-13-90(a)).
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** EEOC quorum restored when Panuccio was sworn in on Oct 27, 2025 [S14]. The Commission rescinded the 2024 harassment guidance 2–1 on Jan 22, 2026 (release dated Jan 23, 2026), and Chair Lucas said this "does not give employers license" to harass [S13]. *Ames v. Ohio Dept. of Youth Services*, decided June 5, 2025, struck the "background circumstances" rule for majority-group plaintiffs [S16].

**How often.** Retaliation was alleged in 54,350 of 88,201 EEOC charges in FY 2025 (61.6%), the most of any basis [S15].

**Recommendation: Include.** Build in: the OWBPA checklist for any release, the Speak Out Act carve-out for confidentiality clauses, retaliation risk when an employer sues or threatens after a protected complaint, and state coverage thresholds.

**Key authorities:** 42 U.S.C. 2000e-3(a) [S10]; 29 U.S.C. 626(f) [S9]; 42 U.S.C. 19403 [S11]; IC 22-9-1-3 [S18]; S.C. Code 1-13-30/-80/-90 [S17].

## 4. IP ownership — Include

**Intersection.** Invention-assignment and work-for-hire clauses sit beside confidentiality and non-compete clauses in most employee contracts. They decide whether the departing employee's code, inventions or designs are the employer's property, which sets up the insider-theft claim.

- **Patents.** An inventor owns the invention by default. *Stanford v. Roche* (June 6, 2011) held that the Bayh-Dole Act does not automatically vest title in federal contractors [S48]. In that case, the inventor "agree[d] to assign" to Stanford, but agreed he "will assign and do[es] hereby assign" to Cetus [S48, syllabus]. The drafting lesson that "hereby assign" works a present transfer comes from Federal Circuit cases not opened here (UNVERIFIED for this file; route to 03). Patent assignments must be in writing, and an unrecorded one can lose to a later purchaser unless recorded within three months [S50] (35 U.S.C. 261).
- **Copyright.** In a work made for hire, the employer "is considered the author" [S49] (17 U.S.C. 201(b)).
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** *Thaler v. Perlmutter* (D.C. Cir., decided Mar 18, 2025) held that the Copyright Act requires work "authored in the first instance by a human being" [S51, pg. 3]. A machine cannot be an author, so material generated entirely by AI may have no copyright for an assignment clause to transfer. The Supreme Court denied certiorari on Mar 2, 2026 (No. 25-449) [S63].
- **IN and SC.** No IN or SC statute limiting employee invention-assignment clauses was found (UNVERIFIED negative; such statutes exist in other states).

**How often.** Opinion counts were too small to use (2 IN opinions since 2016). Assignment clauses are standard in tech and engineering offer letters (no dataset; judgment).

**Recommendation: Include.** Build in: present-assignment wording, carve-outs for prior inventions, work-for-hire scope (it fits employees, not most contractors), AI-generated output, and recording.

**Key authorities:** *Stanford v. Roche*, 563 U.S. 776 (2011) [S48]; 17 U.S.C. 201(b) [S49]; 35 U.S.C. 261 [S50]; *Thaler v. Perlmutter* (D.C. Cir. 2025) [S51].

## 5. Data privacy and breach notification — Include (narrow)

**Intersection.** When an insider walks out with customer or employee personal data, the employer may owe breach notices. The new employer that receives the data may take on obligations too.

- **Indiana CDPA (CHANGED 2025–26).** Effective January 1, 2026 [S39][S40][S41]. It applies to businesses that control or process data of at least 100,000 Indiana consumers, or 25,000 with more than 50% of revenue from selling data [S69][S39] (IC 24-15-1-1; official: IN AG guide [S69]). A "consumer" acts for personal or household purposes and "does not include an individual acting in a commercial or employment context" [S69][S41] (IC 24-15-2-8(b); official in substance: the AG guide says a person "acting as an employee or in a commercial context is not a consumer" [S69]; the quoted wording is FindLaw's [S41]). Applicant and employee data used in that role is exempt [S40] (IC 24-15-1-2(13); FindLaw, secondary (official source not reachable)). The Indiana Attorney General's official CDPA guide independently confirms the Jan 1, 2026 effective date, both thresholds, and that a person acting as an employee is not a consumer [S69]. **The CDPA does not govern employee privacy.** It may matter only when a departing employee takes Indiana consumers' data to a new business that meets the thresholds.
- **Indiana breach law.** A database owner must notify affected Indiana residents and the attorney general, when it knows or should know the breach has resulted or could result in identity deception, identity theft or fraud. It must also notify the consumer reporting agencies if more than 1,000 consumers are notified [S74][S42] (IC 24-4.9-3-1; official in part: the IN AG's business FAQ confirms the AG notice and the more-than-1,000 CRA notice [S74]; the identity-deception trigger rests on FindLaw [S42], secondary (official source not reachable)). Notice is due without unreasonable delay [S74], and "not more than forty-five (45) days" after discovery [S43] (IC 24-4.9-3-3; the 45-day limit is FindLaw, secondary (official source not reachable); the AG FAQ states only "without unreasonable delay"). UNVERIFIED: the exact wording of Indiana's breach definition and its employee good-faith exception (IC 24-4.9-2-2 could not be opened).
- **South Carolina breach law.** Unauthorized access to and acquisition of unencrypted data is a breach. An employee's good-faith acquisition is not a breach if the data is not misused or further disclosed [S44] (S.C. Code 39-1-90(D)(1)). Notice is due without unreasonable delay. Notices to more than 1,000 people also go to the Department of Consumer Affairs and the consumer reporting agencies [S44] (§39-1-90(A), (K)). The administrative fine for knowing and willful violations is $1,000 per resident [S44] (§39-1-90(H)).
- UNVERIFIED: whether SC enacted a comprehensive consumer privacy law in 2025–26. A verifier search found none; secondary reports (not checked at scstatehouse.gov) say SC instead enacted a narrower children's Age-Appropriate Design Code act (H 3431) on Feb 5, 2026.

**How often.** Opinion counts were too small to use. Insider theft of customer lists often includes personal data (judgment; no dataset).

**Recommendation: Include, narrow.** Build in a breach-notice trigger check (IN 45 days plus AG; SC rules) for any insider-theft memo involving personal data. Exclude the CDPA as an employee-privacy law, but flag it for the receiving business.

**Key authorities:** IC 24-15-1-2 and 24-15-2-8 [S40][S41]; IC 24-4.9-3-1 and -3-3 [S42][S43]; S.C. Code 39-1-90 [S44].

## 6. NLRA concerted activity — Defer (flag)

**Intersection.** For non-supervisory employees, overbroad confidentiality, non-disparagement and severance terms can violate Section 7, which protects "other concerted activities" for "mutual aid or protection" [S25] (29 U.S.C. 157). Union membership is not required. Supervisors and independent contractors are not "employees" under the Act [S26] (29 U.S.C. 152(3), (11)), which removes many of the managers and engineers in covenant disputes.

- ***McLaren Macomb*, 372 NLRB No. 58 (Feb 21, 2023).** Merely offering a severance agreement that requires broad waiver of NLRA rights is unlawful. The agreements in the case barred disparagement and disclosure of terms [S27]. The case page shows a circuit decision dated Sept 19, 2024 and a compliance stage in 2026 [S28]. Secondary reports say the Sixth Circuit enforced the Board's order without settling the broader severance question; SECONDARY ONLY, the opinion was not opened.
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** GC 25-05 (Feb 14, 2025) rescinded GC 23-05 (*McLaren Macomb* guidance), GC 23-08 (non-competes violate the NLRA), GC 25-01 (remedies for non-compete and stay-or-pay terms) and GC 23-02 (electronic monitoring) [S29]. The GC's theory that non-competes violate the NLRA is no longer agency policy.
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** GC Crystal Carey was sworn in on Jan 7, 2026 [S36]. In GC 26-04 (Aug 26, 2026), she is arguing to overrule *McLaren Macomb* in *Valley Radiology* and *Stericycle* (work rules) in *Honeywell* [S30, pg. 2]. Regions still apply "extant Board law" until then [S30, pg. 1]. The *Valley Radiology* docket (10-CA-324512) shows exceptions to the ALJ decision filed Mar 31, 2026, briefing through May 19, 2026, and no Board decision as of 2026-10-20 [S66]. The Board now has four members: Chairman Murphy and Mayer (sworn in Jan 7, 2026), Macy (Aug 17, 2026) and Prouty [S32][S33][S34][S35].

**How often.** Private-sector union density was 5.9% in 2025 [S37]. Union membership among all wage and salary workers (public and private) was 8.3% in Indiana and 2.7% in South Carolina [S38]; BLS's state table is not private-sector only. Section 7 also covers non-union workers, so density understates exposure. Opinion counts were too small to use.

**Recommendation: Defer (flag).** Flag when the employee is non-supervisory and the agreement has confidentiality, non-disparagement or severance-waiver terms. Say that *McLaren Macomb* is current law but under challenge, and that the Board composition changed in 2026. Too unstable to build in.

**Key authorities:** 29 U.S.C. 157 and 152 [S25][S26]; *McLaren Macomb* [S27]; GC 25-05 [S29]; GC 26-04 [S30].

## 7. Unemployment eligibility — Defer (flag)

**Intersection.** An employee fired for theft or a rules breach may be denied benefits. The benefits hearing creates an early record of the same facts the covenant or theft case will turn on.

- **Indiana.** "Discharge for just cause" includes knowingly violating a reasonable, uniformly enforced rule, damaging employer property through willful and wanton misconduct, and "any breach of duty" reasonably owed the employer [S57] (IC 22-4-15-1(d); FindLaw, secondary (official source not reachable); a DWD policy cites (d) as the just-cause definition but does not quote it [S75]). Theft is not listed by name [S57].
- **South Carolina.** Misconduct means willful and wanton disregard of the employer's interests. It brings 20 weeks of ineligibility [S58] (§41-35-120(2)(a)). Gross misconduct includes "employee theft of items valued at more than fifty dollars." It disqualifies until the worker is reemployed and earns eight times the weekly benefit [S58] (§41-35-120).
- No 2025–26 change was found (UNVERIFIED negative).

**How often.** Opinion counts were too small to use (0 SC opinions; IN not returned). This is judgment, not data.

**Recommendation: Defer (flag).** Flag that a benefits hearing may come first and that statements made there can be used later.

**Key authorities:** IC 22-4-15-1 [S57]; S.C. Code 41-35-120 [S58].

## 8. Tax treatment of severance and settlements — Defer (flag)

**Intersection.** Severance and settlement terms in separation agreements have tax effects that change net value and drafting.

- Severance payments "are taxable wages for FICA purposes" [S52] (*United States v. Quality Stores*, Mar 25, 2014).
- No deduction is allowed for a sexual-harassment or sexual-abuse settlement, or the attorney fees for it, when the payment is "subject to a nondisclosure agreement" [S53] (26 U.S.C. 162(q)). That ties confidentiality clauses to tax cost.
- Separation pay for involuntary separation is outside 409A only if it is no more than twice the lesser of annualized pay or the 401(a)(17) limit, and is paid by the end of the second year after separation [S54] (26 C.F.R. 1.409A-1(b)(9)(iii)).
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** the overtime deduction (26 U.S.C. 225) affects back-pay and misclassification math for 2025–2028 [S8].

**Recommendation: Defer (flag).** Flag FICA wage treatment, 409A timing on installment severance, and 162(q) when an NDA covers harassment. Send the question to a tax professional, not the memo.

**Key authorities:** *Quality Stores* [S52]; 26 U.S.C. 162(q) [S53]; 26 C.F.R. 1.409A-1(b)(9)(iii) [S54].

## 9. Immigration (H-1B) — Defer (flag)

**Intersection.** Repayment and early-departure clauses are common for sponsored workers, and H-1B rules limit them.

- An employer may not require an H-1B worker to pay "a penalty" for leaving before an agreed date. Bona fide liquidated damages are allowed, and state law decides which is which [S55] (20 C.F.R. 655.731(c)(10)(i)). Deductions may not recoup the employer's business costs, such as petition fees and lawyer fees [S55] (§655.731(c)(9)(iii)(C)). No part of the $500/$1,000 additional fee may be shifted to the worker [S55] (§655.731(c)(10)(ii)). UNVERIFIED: the regulation's dollar figures may lag the current statutory fee; check 8 U.S.C. 1184(c)(9).
- **CHANGED 2025–26:** Proclamation 10973 of Sept 19, 2025 restricts entry of H-1B workers unless petitions come with a $100,000 payment. It took effect Sept 21, 2025 and expires 12 months later "absent extension" [S56]. A Sept 18, 2026 proclamation extended it to 12:00 a.m. EDT on Sept 21, 2027 [S64]. UNVERIFIED (secondary only): reports that federal court orders block the fee's implementing policies.

**Recommendation: Defer (flag).** Flag when the employee holds a visa: early-departure penalties and fee recoupment are barred, and repayment clauses must be real liquidated damages.

**Key authorities:** 20 C.F.R. 655.731 [S55]; Proclamation of Sept 19, 2025 [S56].

## 10. Workplace AI rules — Exclude as a standalone field

**Intersection.** Two real overlaps exist, and both belong to other fields. (a) An employee who pastes trade secrets into a public AI tool, or an employer that lets employees do so, weakens the "reasonable measures" element of a trade secret [S45] (18 U.S.C. 1839(3)(A)); this belongs in ticket 01. (b) AI-generated work may not be copyrightable [S51]; this belongs in IP (section 4).

- **CHANGED 2025–26:** the Jan 23, 2025 order revoked EO 14110 [S46]. It is EO 14179 (90 FR 8741, published Jan 31, 2025), confirmed by the Federal Register API and by EO 14365 §1, which cites it by number [S62][S47]. EO 14365 (Dec 11, 2025) creates an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws and a Commerce list of "onerous" ones. It does not itself preempt state law [S47]. NLRB GC 23-02 on algorithmic management was rescinded [S29].
- No IN or SC statute on AI in hiring or employment was found (UNVERIFIED negative; search budget ran out). UNVERIFIED (secondary only): law-firm reports say the EEOC removed its AI technical-assistance documents from its site on Jan 27, 2025; no EEOC notice was found.

**Recommendation: Exclude as its own field.** Add an AI-tool check to the insider-theft reasonable-measures list (01), and the human-authorship point to IP ownership. Revisit if IN or SC passes an AI employment law.

**Key authorities:** 18 U.S.C. 1839(3) [S45]; EO of Jan 23, 2025 [S46]; EO 14365 [S47].
