## Recommendations
**Before signing**
- (#1, #2) List the three apps on Exhibit A and ask for the §6 carve-out in Path step 2. "You assign" reads like present-transfer wording, the form courts treat as strongest, so the fix has to come before you sign [11]. Talk to a licensed attorney before you act.
- (#3, #4) Limit any phone wipe to a Company work profile [12]. Ask to strike §3 or tie it to your role, direct competitors and your territory; Indiana courts strike words but won't add limits [4][13]. Talk to a licensed attorney before you act.
- (#5, #6) §5 lacks the DTSA immunity notice, so the Company can't get exemplary damages or fees from you [16]. Ask for a pro-rated bonus repayment and no paycheck deduction [18][19]. Talk to a licensed attorney before you act.

**On the job**
- (#2, #4, #5) Keep Company devices, data and hours away from the apps, and log your off-hours work [17][22].

**When leaving**
- (#5, #6) Return the laptop and all Company files. If final pay is docked, ask in writing which statute allows it [18][20].

## Creative option
**A. Creative, not standard practice.** Before signing, assign the apps in writing to a single-member LLC you own, and still list them on Exhibit A. §6 reaches "prior inventions you have", and the LLC would own them instead.
Why it might work: a copyright or patent transfer takes effect by a signed writing [2][21].
**Risk:** Medium: after you leave, owning the LLC could be called owning "any interest" in an analytics business under §3 [4].
**Enforceability:** Uncertain: Jeeves's files found no Indiana appellate case on how far an invention-assignment clause reaches [UNVERIFIED].
Run it past a licensed attorney before using it.

**B. Creative, not standard practice.** Ask the Company to countersign a side letter disclaiming the listed apps and later versions you build off-hours on your own equipment. Exhibit A covers only what exists today.
Why it might work: the employer owns a work made for hire unless a written instrument both parties sign says otherwise [3].
**Risk:** Low: a refusal shows how §6 will be used.
**Enforceability:** Likely if signed, since copyright ownership turns on signed writings [2][3].
Run it past a licensed attorney before using it.
