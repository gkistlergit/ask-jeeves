- §3's "any interest" ban could reach owning your apps if they are analytics products [7][9].
- The October 9, 2026 sign-by date has passed; the November 2 start is 13 days away.

- (#1) Complete Exhibit A; keep a dated copy. Talk to a licensed attorney before you act.
- (#2) Propose this §6 carve-out, draft for your attorney to review: "This assignment excludes the inventions listed on Exhibit A, and improvements to them, developed entirely on my own time without Company equipment, supplies, facilities or Confidential Information." Talk to a licensed attorney before you act.
- (#3) Ask to delete "own any interest"; nationwide and "any capacity" covenants have failed in Indiana [9][11]. Talk to a licensed attorney before you act.
- (#6) Ask that §4 cover only customers you served and employees whose knowledge would help a competitor [7], and that §5 add the whistleblower-immunity notice [15]. Talk to a licensed attorney before you act.

## Creative option
**Creative, not standard practice.** A. Register each app's copyright before signing and cite the numbers in Exhibit A, so the Company countersigns a dated record of your authorship.
Why it might work: registration within five years of publication is prima facie evidence of validity and of the certificate's facts [6].
**Risk:** Low: filing costs; it records only what existed on that date.
**Enforceability:** Likely as evidence, though it does not cover code added during employment, which still needs the carve-out [2][6].
Run it past a licensed attorney before using it.

**Creative, not standard practice.** B. Before signing, assign the apps in writing to a single-member LLC you own and disclose it in Exhibit A; the LLC is not a party to the letter.
Why it might work: copyright moves by a signed writing [2], patent rights by a written instrument recorded within three months [5], and invention rights start with the inventor [4].
**Risk:** Medium: §3's "own any interest" could reach the LLC, and a hidden transfer would look like evasion.
**Enforceability:** Uncertain: no Indiana case tests it [1], and code you write while employed is still caught by §6.
Run it past a licensed attorney before using it.

## Adjacent issues flagged
- IP ownership: work made for hire covers only work within the scope of employment [3]; the rest moves only by a signed writing [2].
- Wage deductions: Indiana needs a signed, revocable assignment for a listed purpose [17], bars fines from wages [18], and equipment charges against an exempt salary can cost the exemption [19].
- Whistleblower: without the DTSA notice the Company loses DTSA exemplary damages and fees against you [15]; §5 can't bar SEC reports [20] or harassment disclosures [21].
- Also noted: the $10,000 signing bonus is repayable in full "for any reason" within 12 months [17].
- NLRA (deferred): broad confidentiality rules are under NLRB review [22, UNVERIFIED].
- Unemployment, tax on severance, immigration (deferred): not raised by this letter.
